Why this matters to the decision. Rent growth in a NNN lease is a contractual formula, not a market outcome. What the area economy governs is the tenant's ability to keep paying it. A restaurant sector that is still adding jobs, in a county whose overall payrolls are flat and whose population is shrinking, is a reasonable place to own a fast-food box — for as long as the lease runs. It is not a reason to bet fourteen years of ownership on it.

Industry detail worth reading twice

  • Education and health services added 37,500 jobs S02 ↗ — effectively the entire county net gain. Growth is defensive and publicly funded, not discretionary consumer spending.
  • Information lost 10,200 jobs S02 ↗, with motion pictures and sound recording down 7,300. For a Central Los Angeles site that depends on studio-adjacent lunch and afternoon traffic, this is the most relevant negative series in the dataset.
  • Restaurants and other eating places added 7,300 jobs S02 ↗ — the tenant's industry is expanding even as the county stalls. This supports renewal, not expansion.
  • Energy CPI +20.3% S03 ↗ — delivery-heavy quick-service economics are more exposed to fuel than dine-in formats.
  • The unemployment rate fell to 4.5% from 5.9%, but the labour force shrank 1.8% and employment fell 0.4% S02 ↗. The improvement is people leaving, not hiring accelerating. Do not read it as strength.

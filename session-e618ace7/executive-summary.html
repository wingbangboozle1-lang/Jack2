Executive summary

This page is the whole answer. It is written to be read on its own; every number in it links back to an official source, and every judgement is labelled as a judgement.

1. The decision

Sell the unexpired lease term, or renew it? The evidence says the question is misframed. The asset's value is not mainly a bet on Central Los Angeles rents — it is a bet on how many years of contracted rent a buyer can see. At today's rate level, moving from ten years of term to one year of term costs about 17.1% of value on identical income (no official sourcemodel table below). Rent growth over the same window, at the local CPI of +3.7% S03 ↗, is worth less than that erosion unless it compounds for many years — and it is not guaranteed.

Recommendation. Run a two-track decision in 2027 and exit before term decays:
  1. Track one — create value: negotiate a ten-year extension at or above the in-place rent, with CPI-linked escalators and no material concessions. An extension is the single cheapest way to add value, because it converts time into contracted cash flow.
  2. Track two — prepare the exit: obtain a broker opinion of value, a parcel-level hazard classification and current insurance terms, and line up a qualified intermediary for a Section 1031 exchange S13 ↗.
  3. Then sell in 2029–2032 with roughly twelve years of term, and roll the proceeds into a diversified net-lease position. This is strategy EXT_SELL: $2,386,488 of risk-adjusted wealth per $100,000 of NOI, against $1,825,966 for doing nothing and $2,398,403 for selling immediately through an exchange. The last two are effectively tied; the choice between them is a choice about how much control to keep.
  4. Fallback: if the tenant will not extend on acceptable terms by mid-2027, sell in 2027–2028 with at least five years of term, through a 1031 exchange. Do not wait for the 2030–2035 expiry window to arrive with the clock running down.

2. Why not simply hold?

Because holding is the only option that takes certain decay in exchange for uncertain growth. In the simulation it has the worst 5th-percentile outcome of all six strategies ($1,127,622 versus $2,157,061 for extend-and-sell) and the lowest risk-adjusted wealth. The mean is not much worse than the alternatives — but the spread is enormous, and the downside is realised precisely in the states of the world where the Owner most needs liquidity: tenant non-renewal, a rate shock, or a hazard event.

3. Why not simply sell today?

Selling today is defensible and much better than holding, but it gives away two things: the value created by an extension you can probably obtain, and the deferral benefit of a 1031 exchange, which is worth roughly $149,040 per $100,000 of NOI in the model. Selling in 2026 also forces the Owner to accept today's bid without a competitive process.

5. The tax facts that shape the exit

  • City transfer tax 0.45% plus county 0.11% S10 ↗ S11 ↗.
  • Measure ULA: 4.0% on City sales above $5,400,000 and 5.5% at $10,900,000 or above, for closings after 2026-06-30, with thresholds indexed to chained CPI S10 ↗. At the model's base cap rate the $5.40m threshold is reached at about $305,100 of annual NOI. The threshold is a cliff, not a phase-in.
  • Proposition 13: a sale resets the assessed base, so the tenant's tax reimbursement rises in year one. That is a renewal risk you create by selling S12 ↗.
  • Capital gains: California's top marginal rate on the 2025 schedules is 12.30% S14 ↗; the federal layers are standard statutory rates and are not separately verified here. The model uses a blended 37% of gain, disclosed as an assumption.
  • Section 1031 is limited to real property and defers the gain rather than eliminating it S13 ↗.

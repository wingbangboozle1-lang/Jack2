What a buyer is actually buying

A single-tenant net-lease asset is a bond with a property attached. The buyer's analysis is: contracted rent times years of term, discounted at a rate that reflects the tenant's credit and the residual real estate risk. That is why the recommendation focuses on term and tenant credit rather than on the neighbourhood's momentum. The three inputs a buyer will underwrite, in order:

  • Years of remaining term — the larger the number, the lower the cap rate, all else equal.
  • Tenant credit — a tenant with negative book equity and a $20.00/hour wage floor is priced as sub-investment-grade (tenant credit).
  • Rent relative to market — below-market rent is a liability at expiry, not an asset.

What is deliberately missing

There is no published official cap rate for a single-tenant quick-service restaurant in Central Los Angeles. Rather than manufacture one, this workbook carries the cap rate as an explicitly labelled assumption (A01, 5.25% at ten years of term, tested from 4.75% to 5.75%) and publishes the whole sensitivity grid. The Owner should replace it with a broker opinion of value and three recent comparable sales before acting (flagged U-14).

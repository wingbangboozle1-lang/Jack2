The three that decide the outcome

1. Term decay (K08). Certain, not probabilistic. Every year of remaining term that passes widens the cap rate a buyer will pay, and nothing the Owner does can reverse it. This is the only risk in the register with a 100% probability.
2. Non-renewal (K01). The tenant has negative stockholders' equity of −$901.3m S07 ↗ and a $20.00/hour California wage floor S16 ↗. A dark building in a purpose-built fast-food box can sit vacant for a year and a half. Modelled at 18 months at half rent.
3. Exit taxes (K14, K16). The Measure ULA cliff and the capital-gains layer are certain, sizeable and timing-sensitive: 4.0% of the whole price above $5.40m S10 ↗, plus roughly 37% of the gain. They do not make selling wrong; they make unstructured selling wrong.

Black swans, honestly

The register includes six black swans and two structural shocks that a normal projection would ignore: a major earthquake, a wildfire-driven urban conflagration, insurer withdrawal, a 1031 repeal, a new transfer tax, a pandemic-scale closure, civil disturbance, and the loss of Proposition 13 protection. Only the January 2025 fire pair in this region has a documented precedent; the rest are carried on judgement and, in the model, through a deliberately coarse hazard assumption (1.0% a year for a >20% impairment, 0.25% for total loss) that is disclosed as an assumption rather than dressed up as data.

The parcel's actual fire and flood classifications are blank, because they require the address, which is withheld for privacy. That is the single most valuable piece of information the Owner could obtain: it converts an unquantified tail into a priceable one.

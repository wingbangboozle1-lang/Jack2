Should this Central Los Angeles Jack in the Box be sold before the lease expires, or renewed?

Short answer: neither extreme. Do not let the lease run to expiry, and do not sell the unexpired term without first finding out what it is worth. Two paths — extend, then sell into the extended term, or sell now through a Section 1031 exchange — land within half a percent of each other on risk-adjusted wealth, and both beat doing nothing by about a third. Holding is the only option that loses on every measure.

The decision in one paragraph. In 2027, ask the tenant for a ten-year extension at or above the current rent with CPI-linked escalators. Run a quiet sale process at the same time. If the extension is signed on acceptable terms, market the property in 2029–2032 with roughly twelve years of term, when the same income is worth materially more than it is today. If the tenant will not extend on acceptable terms by mid-2027, sell in 2027–2028 with at least five years of term and move the proceeds through a Section 1031 exchange. Doing nothing is the only option that loses on every measure.

“Certainty equivalent” is mean wealth adjusted for risk at constant relative risk aversion of 2 — the number an investor would accept for certain in place of the uncertain distribution. A strategy with a high mean but a brutal tail ranks below one with a slightly lower mean and a firm floor. All of this is assumption-driven: see the model for every input, and the sensitivity grid for where the ranking flips.

The one conclusion that survives every sensitivity test. Passive hold is last or second-to-last on both mean and risk-adjusted wealth across the entire 15-cell grid. Term decay is certain; rent growth is not. That asymmetry, not a forecast, is what decides this.

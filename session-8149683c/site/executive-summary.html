The bottom line

Do not sell simply because the lease expires. Lease expiry is a refinancing event, not a liquidation event. The market evidence in this dossier shows net leases re-let at above prior rent: Realty Income disclosed a realised rent recapture rate of 103.5% in Q3 2026 and 103.1% year-to-date (SEC Form 8-K, 2026-11-02).

The real decision is a spread, not a calendar. Hold whenever your required return is below the sell/hold crossover. On the base case (7.10% cap, 6 years left) that crossover is 7.39%. Sell when your required return is above it. Everything else is second-order.

Do not renew at any price. The tenant's own 10-K shows same-store sales of −4.2%, franchise AUV down −3.9%, transactions down −5.7%, 86 closures against 31 openings, and two consecutive years of goodwill impairment. A renewal that requires you to cut rent to keep the box occupied is a concession, not a win, and it should be priced as one.

3. The four decisions, priced the same way

All four are valued with one discounted cash-flow engine: net rent received plus sale proceeds, discounted at the owner's required return. The only differences between branches are the exit date, the exit cap rate, and whether a lease roll happens before the exit. 4. What could actually hurt you

Business as usual

  • Roll risk. If the tenant does not renew, you carry roughly ten months of lost rent plus re-leasing cost and a wider exit cap. Modelled explicitly.
  • Tenant concentration. 100% of your income comes from one brand whose same-store sales have fallen two years running.
  • Rate risk. With the 10-year at 4.90%, every 100 bps of cap-rate expansion removes roughly one seventh of capital value.
  • California labour policy. AB 1228 set a $20.00/hr fast-food floor and the Fast Food Council can raise it. The tenant's own 10-K cites AB 1228 directly for a 7.6% wage increase.
  • Prop 13 reset. Any sale you complete resets the assessed value to market and raises the tenant's property-tax reimbursement — a permanent occupancy-cost increase exactly when the lease is up.

Black swans

  • Wildfire. The January 2025 fires are a direct, recent county-level shock. Your parcel's Fire Hazard Severity Zone is unverified because the address is redacted.
  • Earthquake. Central Los Angeles sits in a high-hazard seismic region. Your site's design spectral acceleration is unverified.
  • Insurance withdrawal. Coverage exists through the FAIR Plan at $20M per building / $100M per location, but at insurer-of-last-resort pricing.
  • A tenant bankruptcy or mass closure programme. The tenant has already run 86 closures in one year and is covenant-constrained by its own securitisation.
  • Population loss. The county is -3.2% smaller than in 2020, and LAEDC forecasts a further -0.2% in 2026.

5. What is still unknown

  1. Is the tenant the public company or a franchisee, and is there a guaranty? (P-02, P-03)
  2. What is the exact expiry date? It sets the remaining term, which sets the price. (P-04, P-05)
  3. What is the current rent, and what is the unit's sales volume? It decides whether the box is relettable and whether the tenant can afford a renewal. (P-07, P-13)
  4. What renewal options exist, and at what rent mechanism? (P-06)
  5. What is the debt, and what would retiring it cost? A sale you cannot complete is not an option. (P-16)

6. Recommended course of action

Sequence, not a single decision
  1. Immediately: pull the five documents above. Until P-02, P-04, P-06, P-07 and P-16 are known, no sale or renewal should be executed. This is the only blocking action.
  2. Check the decline-in-value window. The 2026-27 LA County decline-in-value filing window closes 2026-11-30. If you believe market value sits below your factored base year value, that is a free option that expires in three weeks.
  3. Establish your required return as a number. Write it down. The entire hold/sell decision reduces to comparing it with the crossover on the decision page.
  4. Open an early-extension conversation with the tenant (Decision D), timed to while you still have six or more years of term. Term you sell is worth more than rent you concede, and the buyer market pays for WALT.
  5. Only sell if your required return is above the crossover, or if concentration risk is unacceptable to you regardless of the arithmetic. In that case sell on your timetable, not the lease's, and structure it as a §1031 exchange within 45 days of identification.
  6. Do not default to "sell at expiry." It is the one branch that is dominated in almost every scenario tested: you keep the operating risk without the term, and you sell into a wider cap.

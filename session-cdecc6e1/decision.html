Comments by option

  • D1: Highest wealth preservation and liquidity. Growth capped by the 4.84% Treasury alternative (M53) unless a 7%+ net-lease replacement is found (T11 directional). Tax efficiency is the weak score because the full stack (5.06% DTT + ULA tier) applies absent a successful 1031; California state conformity to 1031 not re-confirmed this session (X-06).
  • D2: Best combined score in most scenarios: extends the tenant's stated renewal practice (M75) and captures a WALT premium while still avoiding the 2030-2035 repricing risk. Execution risk is moderate (option audit P01, extension documentation).
  • D3: Lowest wealth preservation: unpriced cap-rate risk, tenant credit risk (M70/M72), county population decline through 2040 (M03-M06), and insurance/catastrophe risk (R-12/R-13/R-21/R-22) all sit inside the hold. Deferring the tax decision preserves basis step-up considerations but magnifies exposure.
  • D4: Balanced compromise: retains optionality and keeps upside if markets improve, while capping exposure to expiry risk. Wealth preservation and liquidity trail D2 because the sale window is deferred.

Breakeven logic

  • Breakeven reinvestment yield: if sale proceeds net of the full tax stack can earn more than ~4.84% (verified 10-year Treasury, M53) with comparable safety, selling dominates holding on wealth preservation.
  • Net-lease reinvestment anchor: 7.3% initial weighted average cash yield on FY2025 net-lease acquisitions (M55, T11 - directional only). Holding the subject asset is competitive only if its effective yield at exit exceeds roughly 6.0%-6.5% after transaction costs.
  • Holding wins only if: renewal is at market rent (not below-market fixed step-up), cap rate at re-sale stays <= ~6.5%, county population/jobs stabilize, and insurance remains available at reasonable cost.
  • All figures above are pre-tax, non-appraisal scenario analysis. A formal appraisal and CPA tax model (P05, P11) are prerequisites to any binding decision.

# Sell or renew? A net-leased restaurant in central Los Angeles

Decision support for one question: a single-tenant Jack in the Box restaurant in central Los Angeles is leased on
net terms and the lease expires between 2030 and 2035. Should the owner sell before expiry, or renew?

**Live site: https://wingbangboozle1-lang.github.io/Jack2/**

The site at the root of this repository is the **consolidated, re-verified site** (final integration, session
`1c30f8e8`, 2026-11-12). Every earlier workbook is preserved in the archive and is not endorsed by it.

## What the site says, in three lines

1. The public evidence cannot settle "sell or renew" by itself: the rent, the exact expiry date, the options, who the
   tenant is and the owner's tax position are private, and are **blank** on the site.
2. The best-supported first move is to **ask the tenant for an early extension**. A refusal points to selling before
   expiry; an agreement leaves a real choice between keeping a secured income and selling a more valuable lease.
3. The choice is about the bad case, not the average: apart from a plain cash sale, the strategies' typical outcomes
   in the scenario model are close to one another, while their one-in-twenty bad outcomes differ widely. The numbers,
   and the assumptions behind them, are on the Decision analysis and Projections pages.

Not an appraisal, not tax advice, not investment advice.

## The rules the build enforces

- **Every published value was read at its official source on the access date** and is stored with the text that was
  on the page. Every number in a value must literally appear in that text.
- **If a value could not be confirmed from an official source it is blank**, flagged, and listed with how to obtain
  it. Nothing is inferred or estimated.
- **Every row of every evidence table links to an official site.**
- **Numbers written in sentences must be in the evidence cited beside them.**
- **Model settings are either anchors** (identical to a verified row, checked at every build) **or labelled
  assumptions** with a tested range.
- **Nothing personal is published.** The owner is `[OWNER-01]`, the property `[PROPERTY-01]`. The build stops on an
  e-mail address, telephone number, street address, parcel number or the name of an individual.
- **Generated pages are never edited by hand.** The build fails if a root page differs from what the generator writes.

## Verification rules used

- A value is published **only** if it was read from the official source on the access date (2026-11-06) and that source is linked on the same row.
- If no official value exists, the cell is **blank** and the row is listed on `flagged.html`. Nothing is inferred, interpolated or taken from an aggregator.
- Verified inputs are kept in a different JSON block from analyst assumptions (`data/valuation.json`), and assumptions are labelled on the site.
- The build fails if a row carries a value without a source, if a "verified" row is empty, if an internal link is broken, or if a possible personal identifier appears.

## Scope and limitations

This is internal decision support, not an appraisal, not tax advice and not investment advice. The brief did not include the street
address, APN, current rent, exact expiry date, purchase basis or loan terms, so no parcel-level value, hazard classification or tax
computation is asserted. Those gaps are enumerated as P01–P12 and F01–F11 in `flagged.html`.

| | |
|---|---|
| Question | Hold, renew, or sell — with a lease described as expiring anywhere in a five-year window (2030–2035) |
| Method | 20,000-trial Monte-Carlo over five strategies, including a §1031 exchange, scored on **certainty-equivalent after-tax wealth** (CRRA = 3), not on expected value |
| Conclusion | Do not let the lease run to expiry. Run a dual track: pursue an early 10–15 year extension **and** prepare a §1031 sale. Sell either way — the only variable is whether you sell with 13–15 years of term or with 4–9. |
| Headline numbers | Extension is worth **+25.2%** ($12.48 → $15.62 per $1 of NOI); term decay costs **−22.7%**; the expiry ambiguity alone is worth **~21.6% = $500,000** on a $200k-NOI asset |
| Data | 195 rows, 154 verified against T1 official sources, 25 left blank and flagged, every row carrying a source link |
| Privacy | Owner, tenant, address and APN are placeholders; the Owner's income, net worth, bracket and basis are never collected — the model is normalised to $1.00 of NOI |

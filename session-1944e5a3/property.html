What an NNN lease does — and doesn't — protect

  • Landlord receives net rent. Tenant pays taxes, insurance, and maintenance, so owner cash flow is close to NOI with low management burden — attractive for passive wealth preservation.
  • NNN ≠ credit guarantee. If the tenant (or its franchisee) defaults, the landlord keeps the building, not the rent. Re-tenanting a QSR pad is possible but slow; downtime and TI/LC costs can erase 1–3 years of NOI.
  • Value = NOI ÷ cap rate. With flat NOI, value is purely rate-sensitive: if cap rates expand 75–100 bps, price falls roughly 12–20% on the same income.
  • Escalations are the inflation hedge. Inflation protection lives in the bump schedule — verify it keeps pace with LA County CPI (~3%/yr recent pace) or accept value erosion in real terms.
  • Single-asset concentration. One tenant, one pad, one city. The risk register treats this as the defining exposure.

The recommendation

Decision analysis

Six strategies, measured on the same basis, ranked on risk-adjusted after-tax wealth — with the triggers that would change the answer.

Recommended path: secure term, then sell into it. Approach the tenant in 2026 for a 10–15 year extension at or above in-place rent. In parallel, engage a qualified intermediary and identify replacement property. Sell in 2029–2032 with more than ten years of term, through a Section 1031 exchange into a diversified net-lease portfolio. If the extension fails at acceptable rent, sell anyway in 2028. If the tenant credit cracks, sell immediately. Only hold for income beyond 2032 if you are comfortable keeping one building in an earthquake zone on your own balance sheet.

Reading the table

  • The average is not the decision. Holding to expiry and renewing has the highest mean ($4.54m) but a worst 5% of $2.90m. Selling now through an exchange has a lower mean ($4.04m) and the best worst 5% ($3.47m). An owner whose priority is wealth preservation should weight the second number.
  • The tax is bigger than the timing. Selling now with an exchange beats selling now and paying tax by $646,520 per $100,000 of NOI (+19.3%). No amount of market timing in this model matches that.
  • Expiry without renewal destroys value. The go-dark path is the weakest of the six on every measure except none — it is 23% below the leader on the risk-adjusted measure.

What would make the answer different

  • A corporate guaranty or letter of credit. Materially lowers credit risk and pushes the recommendation towards holding for income.
  • In-place rent below market with a fixed-price tenant option. The renewal option becomes a landlord liability; the calculus inverts towards selling before the option is exercised.
  • A very low basis and no appetite to reinvest. A sale becomes a tax event with no offsetting benefit; the hold case improves by roughly the 646,520 difference between S01 and S02 per $100,000 of NOI.
  • Redevelopment value exceeding the net-lease value. None of this analysis applies; the question becomes a land residual, which this workbook does not attempt.

Economy, jobs and wages

Unemployment, payrolls by sector, wage floors, inflation and output for the Los Angeles metro and county.

What the data says. The Los Angeles labour market is healthy at the headline and narrow underneath. Unemployment has fallen to 4.4% in the metro from 5.5% a year earlier, but total payrolls grew only 0.5%. All of the net growth is in education and health (+3.8%) and leisure and hospitality (+1.5%); four private sectors and government shed jobs, with information down 5.7%.

What it means for this property

  • Rent coverage depends on restaurant sales, not on the metro average. Unemployment at 4.4% and consumer prices at +3.7% are supportive of traffic, but the tenant's own same-store sales are negative (tenant page). The economics that matter are the store's, and they are not in this dataset.
  • Wage floors are indexed and rising. California's minimum wage is $16.90, the fast-food floor is $20.00 and the City of Los Angeles floor is $18.42 — each indexed, each applying to a franchisee. Every point of wage inflation comes out of the same margin that pays the rent.
  • Inflation cuts both ways. CPI at +3.7% supports nominal sales and therefore percentage rent, but a lease with fixed 1–2% escalations loses real value every year in this environment. That argues for escalations at or above CPI in any extension.
  • Sector concentration is a local risk. Information employment is shrinking 5.7% a year in a metro that is often described as a media capital. If the immediate trade area depends on one employer or one industry, the store's sales inherit that volatility.

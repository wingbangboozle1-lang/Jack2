Trade-area fundamentals

Market, demographics and schools

Population, income, poverty, housing, education and consumer demand for the City of Los Angeles and Los Angeles County.

What the data says. The trade area is dense, renter-heavy and relatively poor at the city level, inside a county with the largest personal-income base in the United States. Population is falling in the official state projection. That is a stable quick-service market, not a growth market: it supports rent at today's level and does not support underwriting increases.

How to read this against the decision

  • Density and renter share support the drive-thru. 8,304.2 persons per square mile and a 36.0% owner-occupancy rate mean a large, mobile, local customer base. That is why the site is worth re-leasing if the tenant leaves — but re-leasing does not preserve the net-lease premium.
  • Income is split. The city median household income of $81,939 and 16.5% poverty rate sit below the county's $90,112 and 13.3%. A value-oriented fast-food brand fits that profile better than a premium one.
  • Population is a headwind, not a cliff. The DOF projection falls 1.8% from 2026 to 2040 — slow enough that it should not drive a decision, fast enough that it should stop anyone underwriting growth.
  • The school system is large and stable in structure. LAUSD enrols 497,105 students in 2025-26 and 81,328 are English learners. School quality matters for family formation, and the graduation rate could not be confirmed from CDE on the access date — it is blank and flagged rather than estimated.

Note on series: the Census 2025 county estimate (9,694,934) and the CA DOF projection for 2026 (9,811,358) are different series and are not comparable. Both are published here with their labels intact. The Census Bureau itself warns that different vintages are not comparable.

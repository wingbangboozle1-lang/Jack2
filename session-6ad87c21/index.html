Sell before the lease expires, or renew?

A source-verified decision dashboard for a single-tenant Jack in the Box on a triple-net lease in Central Los Angeles, expiring somewhere in 2030–2035.

The answer, in one paragraph. This is a good asset in a slow-growth market with a tenant under pressure and a lease that runs out inside four to nine years. Every year that passes makes the asset worth less at the same income, because buyers pay a wider cap rate for shorter term. The highest risk-adjusted outcome is not a passive hold and not a fire sale: it is to open an extension conversation now, prepare a Section 1031 exit in parallel, and sell in 2029–2032 with more than ten years of term. If the tenant will not extend at or above in-place rent, sell earlier through the exchange rather than let the lease decay. Letting the lease expire without a renewal commitment is the worst of the six options modelled.

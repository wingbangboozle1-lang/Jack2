Executive summary

The decision, the six reasons behind it, the triggers that would change it, and the limits of the evidence.

Decision. Run a dual track from today: (1) approach the tenant and the brand for a 10–15 year extension at or above in-place rent, and (2) prepare a Section 1031 exit so that a sale can close without paying tax twice. Sell in 2029–2032, while more than ten years of term remains. If the extension does not materialise at acceptable rent by the end of 2027, sell anyway — through the exchange — rather than hold into term decay.

Why

  1. Term is the asset. Buyers price a single-tenant net-lease on the years left on the lease. The model charges 25 basis points of extra cap rate for every year below ten years of remaining term, capped at 150 basis points. Selling with six years left instead of twelve is a materially lower price for exactly the same rent.
  2. The tenant is under pressure, and the obligor is probably a franchisee. System same-store sales were −1.1% in Q3 FY2026 after −7.1% a year earlier; guidance includes 50–60 closures; the dividend is suspended; the balance sheet carries $1.47bn of debt against a $901m stockholders' deficit. 93.0% of the 2,115 restaurants are franchised, so the lease credit is a franchisee balance sheet that this workbook cannot see. Tenant page → S07 ↗
  3. The local economy is stable, not booming. Metro unemployment is 4.4% and payrolls are up 0.5% year over year, but growth is concentrated in health care and leisure while information employment falls 5.7%. The state projects the county population falling 1.8% by 2040. That supports the rent; it does not support underwriting growth. S01 ↗ S04 ↗
  4. Rates are the swing factor. The 10-year Treasury fell from 5.31% on 5 October to 4.86% on 6 November 2026, a 45 basis point rally that is worth roughly $310,000 per $100,000 of NOI in the model. That is a reason to be ready to sell into strength, not a reason to assume rates will keep falling. S09 ↗
  5. The tax decides the mechanism, not the timing. A Section 1031 exchange is worth about $670,000 per $100,000 of NOI in terminal wealth versus selling and paying the tax — the single largest controllable number in the whole analysis. It requires 45 days to identify and 180 days to close through a qualified intermediary. S12 ↗
  6. Letting it expire is the worst outcome. Going dark at expiry, re-letting to a local operator at 90% of rent and selling without brand credit produces the lowest certainty equivalent of the six strategies, about 23% below the leader.

What the ranking actually says

The top three strategies are within about six percent of each other on the risk-adjusted measure, which is inside the model's own error. What is not close is the bottom: passive hold to 2040, a taxable sale, and letting the lease expire all sit 20–23% below the leader. So the robust conclusions are the ones that survive the uncertainty:

  • Do not sell without term, if you can convert term first.
  • Do not let the lease run out without a signed commitment.
  • Do not pay the tax if a qualified exchange is available.
  • Do not assume the tenant will renew on favourable terms — get it in writing.

The genuinely uncertain question is whether to sell in 2029–2032 or hold for income to 2036. That is a liquidity and concentration preference, not an arithmetic one: the hold route has a higher average and a higher risk-adjusted value, but its worst decile is about $600,000 lower because it keeps single-tenant, single-site, earthquake-zone risk on the owner's balance sheet for longer. The recommendation above splits that difference.

What this workbook does not know

The brief gave a lease window rather than a date and withheld the address, rent, basis and loan. So this is a decision framework with verified market inputs, not an appraisal. The eleven facts that must be pulled from the lease before a binding decision are listed on the property page, and everything unverified is on the Flagged for Review page with the office that publishes it.

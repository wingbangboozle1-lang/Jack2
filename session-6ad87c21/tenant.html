Credit risk

Tenant and brand credit

What the latest SEC filing actually says about the brand, the franchisees, the closure programme and the balance sheet.

The most important sentence on this site. Jack in the Box Inc. owns and operates only 149 of its 2,115 restaurants. The other 1,966 — 93.0% — are franchised. A lease on a franchised unit is almost certainly with a franchisee entity, whose credit this workbook cannot see. Read everything below as background on the brand and the system, not as the tenant's balance sheet.

What this means for a landlord

  • Closures are a programme, not an accident. The brand guides to 50–60 closures in FY2026 after 86 in FY2025, under a plan it calls "JACK on Track", which also includes selling real estate. A single unit can be closed for portfolio reasons that have nothing to do with its own sales.
  • Real-estate sales by the tenant compete with you. When the brand sells property it is, in effect, a seller of net-lease product in the same market at the same time. That is a negotiation fact, not a financial one.
  • Leverage is high but improving. $1.47bn of debt against a $901m stockholders' deficit, with $46m of cash and the dividend suspended. Net earnings for the first 40 weeks of FY2026 were positive at $27.9m, against a loss in the prior-year period — a genuine improvement that should be stated alongside the deficit, not instead of it.
  • Refinancing is done for now. The company completed a $500m 2026-1 Class A-2 issuance with an anticipated repayment date in May 2031 and repaid its 2019-1 notes. Near-term refinancing risk is lower than it was.
  • What to ask for. Unit-level sales, the franchisee's financial statements, the guaranty, and a written statement that the unit is not on a closure list. Without those, the lease credit is ununderwritten.

The two-sided view

Case for the tenant performing

  • Same-store sales improved from −7.1% to −1.1% year over year.
  • Net earnings turned positive in the first 40 weeks of FY2026.
  • Refinancing completed; no 2026 maturity wall.
  • Drive-thru sites are scarce and the brand needs its best units.

Case against

  • Negative same-store sales and a guided low-single-digit decline.
  • 50–60 closures guided for one year, mostly franchised.
  • Dividend and buyback suspended; negative book equity.
  • Franchisee-level margin fell to 37.4% from 39.3% a year earlier, with higher bad debt.
  • 93% franchisee-operated: the brand's health is not the obligor's health.

The model does not try to resolve this. It prices it: a 35% chance that a hold strategy falls into the no-renewal path, and a credit spread inside the cap rate. Both are stated assumptions, both are published, and both can be replaced with the franchisee's actual financials.

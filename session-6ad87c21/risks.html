Risk register

19 ordinary-course risks and 12 black-swan events, each mapped to the decisions it affects, with an official anchor where one exists.

How to read this register. Every risk is mapped to the decisions it actually affects: SELL (sell now through a 1031), EXTEND (extend then sell), HOLD (hold to expiry and renew), and EXPIRE (let it expire with no commitment). Likelihood and impact are graded judgements, not probabilities, except where an official measured anchor exists and is linked. The register is not a list of things that might happen — it is a list of things that would change the decision.

The three that dominate

  1. Term decay (R01) is certain, not probable. It happens in every scenario in which nothing is done, and it is the only risk on the list that the owner can eliminate without predicting anything. Its mitigation is the recommendation.
  2. Earthquake (R20) is the largest single-event exposure: a 60% chance of an M≥6.7 earthquake in the Los Angeles region within 30 years, and a 31% chance of M≥7.5 S16 ↗. It cannot be diversified inside one building. It can be insured, retrofitted, or sold — and a 1031 exchange into several properties in several markets is the only structural transfer of that risk available to a single-asset owner.
  3. Tenant credit (R03, R04) is the risk most likely to arrive early. 93% of the system is franchised and the brand is closing 50–60 units a year. The owner's exposure is not to Jack in the Box's brand equity; it is to one franchisee's balance sheet.

What is not on this register

Events that would affect the owner but not the decision: a change in the owner's own tax residence, a change in family circumstances, or a desire to hold the asset for reasons that are not financial. Those are real and they outrank everything on this page, but they are not risks to the property and this workbook does not model them.

Valuation, tax & transaction costs

What actually determines net proceeds

A sale price is not proceeds. Four deductions stand between the two, and three of them are set by statute:

  1. Brokerage and closing costs — modelled at 2.0% of price.
  2. City of Los Angeles real property transfer tax — 0.45% of consideration ($2.25 per $500).
  3. Measure ULA — an additional 4% on sales between $5.4 million and $10.9 million, and 5.5% at or above $10.9 million, for closings after 30 June 2026. The thresholds are indexed annually to Chained CPI.
  4. Federal and California income tax on the gain, unless the transaction is a §1031 exchange.

The ULA thresholds are the single most important planning number in the transaction. A sale at $10.9 million pays $648,550 in city transfer tax before the county's documentary transfer tax and before any income tax. A sale at $5.3 million pays $23,850. Structuring a sale — or pricing it — without reference to those thresholds would be an expensive oversight.

Proposition 13 cuts both ways

Under Article XIII A, the general levy is 1% of assessed value (Revenue and Taxation Code §93(b): $4 per $100 at a 25% assessment ratio), and the assessed value can rise by no more than 2% a year (Revenue and Taxation Code §51). A change of ownership generally resets the base-year value.

For a long-held asset this produces a large gap between the owner's tax bill and the buyer's. That gap is why a seller cannot market the property on the current tax number: the buyer will underwrite the reassessed bill, which is roughly 1% of the purchase price, plus bond and assessment levies on top. The model uses only the statutory 1% and therefore slightly understates the buyer's cost — a conservative error in the seller's favour.

If the market falls, the Assessor's decline-in-value (Proposition 8) programme and the disaster-relief programme are both live routes to a lower assessment. The 2026-27 decline-in-value filing window opened on 2 July 2026 and closes on 30 November 2026.

Federal tax: why basis decides everything

Nonresidential real property is depreciated over 39 years, straight line, mid-month convention. Land is not depreciable, and bonus depreciation does not reach the building because it is limited to property with a recovery period of 20 years or less.

On sale, the depreciation already deducted comes back as unrecaptured section 1250 gain, taxed at a maximum 25% rate, with long-term capital gain at up to 20% and any net investment income tax on top.

A §1031 exchange defers the gain by carrying the old basis into the replacement property. It does not forgive it. Since 2018, §1031 applies to real property only — which is why a diversified net-lease position (directly or through a Delaware statutory trust) qualifies, and a portfolio of securities does not.

The practical consequence: the after-tax comparison between selling and holding depends on adjusted basis, which was not supplied. This project therefore reports pre-tax wealth and treats tax as a documented overlay rather than inventing a basis. If the owner's basis is very low, the §1031 exchange is not optional — it is the difference between a good outcome and a taxable one.

Market evidence: the spread that drives the recommendation

No government agency publishes single-asset cap rates. This project did not purchase data. What it can do is anchor to two official disclosures:

  • The 10-year Treasury at 4.84%, which sets the floor for any long-duration yield.
  • Realty Income's 2025 acquisitions at a 7.3% initial weighted-average cash yield on $6.3 billion invested, with same-store rental revenue growth of 1.3%. That is a published, SEC-filed benchmark for what diversified net-lease capital was actually buying at.

Against that, the model assumes PARCEL-01 trades at a 6.00% going-in cap. That assumption is doing real work in the analysis and is flagged as such. It implies a 116 bp spread over the 10-year and a 130 bp discount to diversified net-lease yields — the inversion that makes selling and exchanging accretive on day one.

Two consequences follow honestly:

  • If the property actually trades at 7.0%, the sell-now advantage narrows sharply. The sensitivity table on the Projection page shows the result at 5.5%, 6.0% and 6.5% going-in caps.
  • The only way to replace the assumption is a broker opinion of value. That is gate G3 on the Decision page.

Rent comparables: not available, not invented

There is no official source for central-LA QSR rents. The model therefore never uses a dollar rent. It works entirely in escalators, market-reset ratios and multiples of NOI. Any rent figure in a real offering memorandum would have to come from a broker's comp set, which this project does not have and will not fabricate.

Verified data

The rules that convert a sale price into net proceeds, and the market evidence that brackets what a buyer would pay. California and Los Angeles both tax the transfer, and federal law taxes the gain — so the headline price is not the number that matters.

How to read the status column

  • Verified read directly from an official publisher; link given.
  • Derived arithmetic on verified numbers; formula in the note.
  • Reported official body, read through secondary coverage; caveat printed and listed on the Flagged page.
  • Owner input stated by the owner, no public record; never counted as verified.
  • Unverified could not be confirmed; value deliberately left blank.
  • Withheld removed on purpose for privacy.

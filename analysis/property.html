Property & lease profile

What is known, and how little that is

The asset is a single-tenant, triple-net quick-service restaurant in Central Los Angeles, leased to a Jack in the Box operator, with a lease expiry somewhere in the 2030–2035 window. That is the entire verified universe of property-specific fact, and even it rests on the owner's own description rather than on a document.

Everything else that normally drives a net-lease valuation is absent:

  • the lease, its amendments, its renewal options and any guaranty;
  • rent, escalators, expense caps and contingent-rent provisions;
  • the identity of the tenant of record (franchisee or corporate);
  • building area, land area, drive-thru, parking ratio and condition;
  • the parcel number, zoning, general-plan designation and any entitlement upside;
  • adjusted basis, purchase price and the current tax bill.

This is not a gap that research can close. It is private information that was deliberately not supplied, and reconstructing it from public records would both defeat the privacy requirement and risk publishing identifying detail. The dataset therefore records each missing item as unverified with the reason, and the model is built to work without them.

Why the tenant question is the pivot

The single most consequential unknown is whether PARCEL-01's tenant is the Jack in the Box corporation or a franchisee. The franchisor's own filings settle the base rate: at 5 July 2026 the system had 2,115 restaurants, of which 1,966 — 93.0% — were franchised. Company-operated units were 149.

If the tenant of record is a franchisee, the owner's credit exposure is a small business whose rent is a fixed cost against a single store's sales. That store's sales environment is not healthy: system same-store sales fell 4.2% in FY2025 and 7.4% in the fourth quarter of FY2025, recovering only to (1.1%) in the third quarter of FY2026. Franchised restaurant average unit volume was $1.874 million in FY2025, down from $1.949 million. Meanwhile the franchisor closed 86 units in FY2025 and guided to another 50–100 closures in FY2026, most of them franchised.

That combination — a small operator, falling volumes, and a franchisor actively pruning its estate — is the reason the risk register puts a 5%-a-year hazard on tenant default. It is also the reason a buyer will underwrite the same thing, and price it.

The lease length is the pricing lever

A net-lease asset is bought for its remaining term. Roughly five years of term is the line below which the buyer pool narrows sharply: 1031 buyers, the largest single pool of small-asset buyers, want long weighted-average lease term, and lenders shorten amortisation on leases that roll inside the loan term.

If the lease expires in 2030, the asset is already inside that window today. If it expires in 2035, there are eight to nine years left — enough to sell well, but only if the sale happens soon. Every year of delay consumes the thing being sold.

Document request before any listing

The Decision page carries the full gate list. The property-level minimum is: the lease and all amendments, any guaranty, a tenant estoppel, the current rent roll and CAM reconciliations, the last three years of property tax bills, a Phase I, an ALTA survey, and a seismic (probable maximum loss) study.

How to read the status column

  • Verified read directly from an official publisher; link given.
  • Derived arithmetic on verified numbers; formula in the note.
  • Reported official body, read through secondary coverage; caveat printed and listed on the Flagged page.
  • Owner input stated by the owner, no public record; never counted as verified.
  • Unverified could not be confirmed; value deliberately left blank.
  • Withheld removed on purpose for privacy.

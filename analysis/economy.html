Economy, labour & rates

Slow growth, not recession

The Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim MSA had 6,282,100 nonfarm payroll jobs in September 2026, up 0.5% year on year. Unemployment was 4.4%, down from 5.0% in July. That is a soft but not contracting labour market — consistent with an economy that is growing below the national rate.

The sector detail matters more than the total for a restaurant:

  • Leisure & hospitality added jobs (+1.5%), and at 782,000 it is the sector whose workers buy QSR lunches.
  • Education & health services was the strongest major sector (+3.8%), and it is the most recession-resistant employer in the region.
  • Information fell 5.7% — the media and tech contraction is real and it hollows out the office-adjacent lunch trade that central-LA restaurants depend on.
  • Financial activities (1.7%), government (1.1%) and professional & business services (0.5%) were all flat to down.
  • Trade, transportation & utilities (+0.3%) and construction (0.0%) were stable.

The picture is a two-speed economy: health care and hospitality growing, white-collar and media shrinking. For a burger chain in central LA, that is roughly neutral on traffic and negative on the office-worker daypart.

Inflation is running hotter than the tenant's pricing power

CPI-U for Los Angeles rose 3.7% in the 12 months to September 2026. Food away from home rose 2.8% — below headline. Energy rose 20.3%, with gasoline up 30.8%.

Energy is the more interesting number. A drive-thru QSR is an energy-intensive format, and its customers drive to it. A 30.8% gasoline increase suppresses exactly the trip that the site's format depends on, while raising the tenant's utility and distribution costs. The tenant's ability to pass that through is capped by the 2.8% menu inflation it actually achieved.

Add a contractual rent escalator of 2–3% and the margin squeeze is structural: rent rises faster than price, price rises slower than cost.

Rates have turned against real estate

This is the most important macro fact in the project.

  • The FOMC raised its target range by 25 bp to 3.75%–4.00% on 17 September 2026, after three cuts in 2025.
  • The 10-year Treasury was 4.84% on 5 November 2026, up from 4.19% on 2 January 2026.
  • The 30-year was 5.25% and the 20-year 5.27%: the curve has re-steepened at the long end.

Cap rates are a spread over long Treasuries. A 6.00% going-in cap on a Los Angeles QSR is a 116 bp spread — thin by the standards of the past decade. If long rates keep rising, that spread normalises by pushing values down. On a 6.00% cap, each +50 bp of cap-rate expansion removes about 7.7% of value; +100 bp removes about 14.3%.

The owner cannot control rates. The owner can control whether they are holding a repricing asset when the repricing happens.

Transit is the one clearly positive long-run catalyst

Metro's D Line subway extension opened its first section on 8 May 2026, adding underground stations at Wilshire/La Brea, Wilshire/Fairfax and Wilshire/La Cienega. Sections 2 and 3 — Beverly Hills, Century City, Westwood and UCLA — remain under construction.

For central-LA parcels near the Wilshire corridor, this is genuine long-run accessibility improvement. It supports land value and it supports the redevelopment-option argument. It does not arrive in time to change a 2030 lease rollover, and it does not change the tenant's same-store sales this year.

Labour cost regulation: an unverified input

California's sector-specific fast-food wage floor is widely reported at $20.00/hour since April 2024, with no Fast Food Council increase since. The Department of Industrial Relations pages consulted during this pass returned errors, so no figure is asserted as verified and none is used in any calculation. It is listed as an open item because it is a first-order driver of franchisee profitability and therefore of renewal probability.

How to read the status column

  • Verified read directly from an official publisher; link given.
  • Derived arithmetic on verified numbers; formula in the note.
  • Reported official body, read through secondary coverage; caveat printed and listed on the Flagged page.
  • Owner input stated by the owner, no public record; never counted as verified.
  • Unverified could not be confirmed; value deliberately left blank.
  • Withheld removed on purpose for privacy.

R01Interest rates stay high, so the same rent buys a lower price
The 10-year Treasury was 4.90% on 2026-11-04 and 4.84% on 2026-11-05, after ranging 4.84%-5.31% through October-November 2026, with the Fed funds upper bound held at 4.00% since 2026-09-17. Cap rates typically move with the long rate. At $100,000 of NOI, a move from a 6.00% to a 7.00% cap takes value from $1,666,667 to $1,428,571: a $238,096 (14.3%) loss with no change in rent.
SELLlikelymajorS05 ↗Sell into a priced market with a long WALT rather than waiting for rates to fall; or hold and accept that the asset is a bond substitute whose price will move with rates. Model this explicitly as a cap-rate sensitivity band, never a point estimate.
R02Rent does not keep up with the tenant's own cost inflation
LA metro CPI-U rose 3.7% year over year in September 2026, and the LA-Long Beach CSA Employment Cost Index rose 3.9%. A lease with a flat or 1.0-1.5% fixed escalator loses real ground every year. A 2.0% escalator against 3.7% CPI loses roughly 1.7% of real rent annually, about 8% of real rent over five years.
HOLDlikelymoderateS01 ↗Tie the reset to CPI with a floor and a cap (blend-and-extend), or sell the under-escalated lease and let a buyer reprice it.
R03Office-using employment in the trade area keeps shrinking
Professional and business services shed 5.2 thousand jobs (-0.5%) and the information sector shed 11.4 thousand (-5.7%) in the 12 months to September 2026. A lunch-driven QSR location is exposed to weekday office headcount.
HOLDpossiblemoderateS01 ↗Underwrite on residential and all-day traffic, not on office capture. Verify the trade area composition before extending; if the site depends on office lunch, shorten the term you are willing to lock.
R04Double-digit energy inflation cuts drive-thru traffic and ups delivery cost
CPI-U energy rose 20.3% year over year in the LA metro area in September 2026, versus 3.7% for all items.
HOLDpossiblemoderateS01 ↗Prefer assets on transit corridors and in dense residential catchments; avoid extending a term on a location whose sales model is purely drive-thru.
R05Wage floor creep compresses franchisee margins and their ability to pay rent
The California minimum wage is $16.90 per hour from 2026-01-01 and is indexed annually to CPI-W with a 3.5% annual cap. The AB 1228 fast-food minimum is $20.00. The City of Los Angeles rate is $18.42 from 2026-07-01 and is indexed to LA-area CPI-W. The employer must satisfy the highest applicable rate.
RENEW/EXTENDlikelymoderateS07 ↗Price the lease to the tenant's ability to pay (rent-to-sales), seek a franchisee or corporate guaranty, and prefer a longer term with a known rent schedule over a short term that must be re-let into rising labour costs.
R06Tenant credit deteriorates even while revenue is stable
The tenant reported a fiscal 2025 net loss of $80.7 million and stockholders' equity of -$901.3 million at 2026-07-05. It has since returned to profit (+$27.9 million in the nine months to 2026-07-05) and reduced long-term debt to $1.492 billion from $1.603 billion, so the credit picture is improving, not collapsing.
EXTENDpossiblemajorS18 ↗If the tenant's credit is weak, the value of your lease is capped: buyers price tenant credit. Use the improving trend to negotiate now, while the story is good, rather than after a bad quarter.
R07Unit closure or brand exit leaves a purpose-built vacancy
A single-tenant QSR box is a specialised improvement. If the tenant closes the unit rather than renewing, the owner inherits a building with a drive-thru on a parcel whose highest and best use may be a different operator, a re-tenanting cost, and a rent gap.
HOLDpossibleseverestated in briefThis is the dominant risk of passive holding. Never hold through expiry without either a signed renewal or a live sale process; check whether the lease requires the tenant to restore the premises or remove trade fixtures.
R08Property tax reassessment on sale
Under Proposition 13 the levy is capped at 1% of assessed value and increases are capped at 2% a year, but a change of ownership resets assessed value to the sale price. A sale at $1.43 million on an asset assessed at, say, $600,000 would roughly double the annual tax bill for the buyer. That cost is capitalised into the price the buyer will pay.
SELLalmost certainmoderateS23 ↗Value the asset on an after-reassessment basis when comparing hold and sell. Model the buyer's post-sale tax step-up explicitly; it is one reason a long-held asset cannot simply be priced at a market cap rate.
R09Measure ULA transfer tax crossing a threshold
For transactions closing after June 30, 2026 the ULA thresholds are $5,400,000 and $10,900,000. At or above $5.4 million the City rate becomes 4.45% (base 0.45% + 4% ULA). On a $6 million sale the ULA tax alone is $240,000 versus zero just below the threshold. The thresholds are indexed annually by chained CPI, so they drift upward.
SELLunlikelymoderateS11 ↗If the asset is near the threshold, either size the transaction to stay below it or structure the exit so the tax is priced into the negotiating range. Model it; do not assume it does not apply.
R101031 exchange fails on a deadline and the tax is triggered anyway
Section 1031 requires written identification within 45 days and receipt of replacement property within 180 days, or by the return due date if earlier. Missing either deadline makes the sale taxable; the deferral cannot be fixed after the fact.
SELLpossiblemajorS12 ↗Engage a qualified intermediary before closing, pre-identify more than one candidate property, and never let the 45-day window pass without a signed identification. Treat the deadline as a hard constraint on the sale timetable.
R11Reinvestment risk: the proceeds earn less than the property did
Deploying sale proceeds at 4.84-4.90% (the current 10-year Treasury) will not replace a property yielding 6-7% on cost. Selling only outperforms holding if the proceeds are reinvested at a yield at or above the breakeven the model computes.
SELLpossiblemajorS05 ↗Do not sell before the replacement pipeline exists. Use the breakeven reinvestment yield from the model as a hard gate on the sell decision.
R12Inflation erodes the real value of the sale proceeds
With CPI at 3.7%, cash sitting between sale and reinvestment loses purchasing power at that rate. A six-month gap between closing and redeployment costs roughly 1.8% of real value.
SELLlikelymoderateS01 ↗Pre-identify replacement property and use a QI so the exchange window overlaps with the marketing period.
R13The five-year expiry window is itself a value leak
The lease is described as expiring somewhere between 2030 and 2035. A buyer cannot underwrite a term it cannot measure, so an ambiguous expiry is priced as the worst case or as a discount. Two identical rents, one with 9 years of term and one with 4, do not trade at the same cap rate.
DEFERalmost certainmajorstated in briefDetermine the exact expiry date from the lease and the option notice provisions. Then either extend to a known long term or sell with a defined WALT. Making the expiry certain is free value.
R14Landlord obligations hidden inside an 'NNN' label
Many chain QSR leases are labelled triple net but push roof, structure, parking lot resurfacing and environmental remediation back onto the landlord. Those are exactly the capital items that surface in the last five years of a lease.
HOLDpossiblemoderatestated in briefRead the maintenance and capital-replacement articles line by line before extending. Budget for the items that are the landlord's; a roof replacement can equal a year of rent.
R15Insurance cost escalation even where the tenant pays
After the January 2025 fires the Insurance Commissioner approved a $1 billion FAIR Plan assessment - the first in over 30 years - with member insurers permitted to recoup part of it through a temporary policy surcharge. Costs are being pushed into premiums across the state.
HOLDlikelymoderateS26 ↗In a true NNN lease the tenant pays, but the owner should confirm the tenant is actually maintaining the required coverage and that the lease survives a lapse. If the owner carries any coverage, get a current quote before extending.
R16Concentration: one tenant, one parcel, one metro
A single-tenant asset concentrates tenant credit, building age, parcel-specific hazard and metro-level economic risk in one position. There is no diversification inside the holding.
HOLDalmost certainmajorstated in briefEither accept concentration deliberately with a long term and a strong guaranty, or diversify via a 1031 exchange into several properties or a DST interest. This is the strongest structural argument for selling.
R27Exact expiry unknown means the owner may not control the outcome
If the tenant holds a renewal option, the tenant - not the owner - decides whether the lease continues at the end of the term. The owner's 'renew or sell' framing assumes a choice the owner might not have.
DEFERpossiblemajorstated in briefRead the option language. If the tenant controls renewal, the only way to control the exit is to sell before the option window opens, i.e. with 5-plus years of term remaining.
R28Measurement and model risk
All forward values in this workbook come from cap-rate and growth assumptions, because no official source publishes asset-level cap rates, the owner's rent, or tax position. A model built on assumptions can be internally consistent and still wrong.
SELLlikelymoderatestated in briefEvery assumption is labelled, published next to the value it produces, and stress-tested across a band. Replace assumptions with an appraisal and a signed lease before acting.

Black swan events

These are the events that would not change the cap rate - they would change whether there is a building, a tenant, or an insurance market. They are the strongest argument for diversification, and the reason the concentration risk counts once on paper and twice in practice.

R17Major earthquake - regional, 60% over 30 years
USGS UCERF3 assigns the Los Angeles region a 60% probability of an M6.7+ earthquake within 30 years, 46% for M7.0 and 31% for M7.5. Over a 5-9 year holding window the probability is lower but not negligible. For an unreinforced or soft-story commercial building, the physical and business-interruption loss can exceed the equity.
HOLDpossiblesevereS24 ↗Obtain a seismic evaluation and retrofitting estimate before extending. Confirm earthquake coverage and its deductible; a 10-15% of value deductible is common and can consume years of rent. In a sale, a buyer will perform exactly this diligence, so fixing it raises price.
R18Wildfire reaching the parcel or its trade area
The January 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires burned 37,469 acres, destroyed 16,251 structures, killed 31 people and were estimated to cost $5.2-10.1 billion in lost economic output and $900 million-$1.6 billion in tax revenue. The fires were not in a Central LA commercial corridor, but the insurance and labour-market shock was regional.
HOLDunlikelysevereS25 ↗Confirm the parcel's Fire Hazard Severity Zone (a local, City of Los Angeles designation for this parcel, not a CAL FIRE SRA zone), maintain defensible space if any, confirm coverage and business-interruption terms, and price the asset on the assumption that insurance is obtainable but expensive.
R19Insurance market withdrawal making the asset uninsurable or unsellable
A $1 billion FAIR Plan assessment after two fires, with recoupment surcharges allowed, shows how quickly the market can reprice catastrophe risk. A buyer's lender will require insurance; no insurance means no financing, and no financing means no buyer at a normal cap rate.
SELLunlikelymajorS26 ↗Obtain a binding quote before extending or listing. Retain the quote. If the asset cannot be insured at a reasonable cost, that is itself a sell signal, and it argues for selling while coverage is still obtainable.
R20Severe drought-driven water restrictions affecting restaurant operations
Regional water restrictions can constrain food-service operations and increase utility costs. Quantitative local restriction data was not verified in this session.
HOLDpossiblemoderatestated in briefConfirm the lease allocates water cost and any rationing risk to the tenant; check the property's water service capacity if the tenant proposes a remodel.
R21Pandemic or civil-order event closing dining rooms
A 2020-type event can close or restrict operations for months. Rent abatement and force-majeure language determines whether the owner is paid during the closure.
HOLDrareseverestated in briefConfirm the casualty/force-majeure clause and whether business interruption insurance is assigned to the landlord. Diversified net-lease REIT exposure absorbs this better than a single asset does.
R22Tenant bankruptcy or a broad unit-closure programme
The tenant has run losses (fiscal 2025 net loss $80.7 million) and carries a stockholders' deficit of $901.3 million, although fiscal 2026 to date is profitable (+$27.9 million). Chains under stress close the weakest units first; a location that is not a top performer is exactly the one at risk.
EXTENDunlikelysevereS17 ↗Ask for unit-level sales when negotiating. A tenant that shares sales data is signalling it wants the site; a refusal is information too. Seek a guaranty or a lease termination payment schedule.
R23Local government fiscal stress raising fees and assessments
The county estimated $900 million-$1.6 billion of tax revenue was lost to the two January 2025 fires. Revenue pressure historically shows up as higher fees, assessments and transfer taxes rather than as a single levy.
SELLpossiblemoderateS25 ↗Model transfer costs at current statutory rates (county 0.11%, City 0.45% plus ULA where applicable) and assume the ULA thresholds, not the rates, are what change.
R24Interest-rate shock re-pricing all net-lease assets
The 10-year moved from 5.31% to 4.84% within about a month in October 2026. A 100 basis point move in the cap rate is roughly a 14% swing in value at a 6-7% cap. A sudden 150-200 bp move would swamp any rent growth the owner could negotiate.
SELLunlikelysevereS05 ↗Lock the sale process while the rate is known, or structure the hold so that a rate shock does not force a sale at the bottom. Never hold with a loan maturity that coincides with a rate spike.
R25Eminent domain, condemnation or a major public-works taking
A street widening, transit project or freeway interchange can take a portion of a corner parcel, eliminating the drive-thru or reducing parking below what the brand requires.
HOLDrareseverestated in briefCheck the City's general plan, specific plan and any active capital projects along the frontage before extending. A short remaining term is an advantage if a taking is plausible.
R26Environmental contamination discovered on the parcel
A former dry cleaner, gas station or auto use on or adjacent to the parcel can make a restaurant redevelopment infeasible and can trigger reporting obligations on a sale.
HOLDrareseverestated in briefObtain a Phase I environmental site assessment before extending or listing. Phase I is a standard and inexpensive step that a buyer will require anyway.

The three that actually decide this

R07 - the unit closes instead of renewing. A purpose-built QSR box with a drive-thru is a specialised improvement. If the tenant walks, the owner inherits downtime, re-fitting capital and a rent gap. This single risk is why "hold to expiry and see" is not a strategy.
R13 - the five-year expiry window. A buyer cannot underwrite term it cannot measure. Making the expiry certain - by extending it or by pricing it - is free value, and it is the one risk the owner can eliminate without spending money.
R16 - concentration. The model values a single asset and a diversified portfolio identically because both are modelled as an income stream. They are not the same risk. A 1031 exchange removes the concentration and defers the tax that would otherwise make diversifying expensive.

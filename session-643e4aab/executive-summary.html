Executive summary

The answer, the reasoning, the two reasons to sell anyway, and what to do in the next 90 days.

For the owner. Six things you need, in the order they matter.

1. The recommendation

Do not drift to expiry. Control the term, then decide. Open a renewal conversation now around a market reset and a longer term. Run a sale process in parallel, priced on a 1031 exchange. Sell if the achievable price implies a cap rate at or below 7.18% - the point at which the replacement asset you could buy stops beating holding. If the achievable cap rate is above that, keep the asset and take the reset.

This is not a hedge, it is a sequence: the extension conversation is the same conversation as the sale, because both are priced off the same thing - remaining term. Signing a long extension raises the sale price; it does not foreclose the sale.

2. Why the financial answer looks like "hold"

On the model, holding to expiry produces $4,484,252 per $100k of NOI by 2040 against $4,212,704 for a 1031 sale today - a gap of $271,548. Across 10,000 draws over cap rates, rent growth, renewal probability and the expiry year, holding wins 67.2% of the time. A taxable sale never wins: it gives up $315,520 of value on day one between costs and tax, and the model shows the deferral is what makes an exit competitive at all.

That result rests on one assumption the model cannot verify: that the tenant renews at market. If it does not, the same model gives the re-let path $4,230,354, and the advantage shrinks. The tenant's own stated renewal practice is on the flagged list precisely because it is the highest-leverage sentence in the 10-K and could not be re-read this session.

4. What the local economy actually says

Better than the headline reputation, and not strong enough to change the decision:

  • Labour market has tightened. City unemployment was 4.5% in September 2026, down from 5.9% a year earlier, and now below the state's 5.1% and only half a point above the national 4.0%. S01 ↗
  • The job mix is shifting, not shrinking. Education and health added 49.1k jobs and leisure/hospitality added 11.5k; information lost 11.4k and professional services lost 5.2k. A lunch-driven office location is the exposure; an all-day residential catchment is not. S01 ↗
  • Population is projected to decline. The state's official projection has Los Angeles County falling from 9,853,435 in 2025 to 9,637,524 in 2040, a 2.19% decline. This is a real headwind for unit volume and it is the single best argument against a 15-year lock-in at flat rent. S04 ↗
  • Income and demand are high in absolute terms - county personal income of $818.5bn is the largest of any US county - but the city median household income ($81,939) trails the county ($90,112), and 16.5% of city residents are in poverty. A value QSR fits that profile; a premium repositioning does not. S03 ↗S20 ↗

5. Your actual next 90 days

  1. Pull the lease and amendments. Exact expiry, every renewal option, the notice dates, the escalation formula, the percentage-rent clause, the maintenance allocation (roof, structure, parking lot) and the casualty clause. Six of the 25 blank rows close with this one document.
  2. Price the parcel's hazards. City of LA fire hazard severity zone, FEMA flood panel, a seismic screening and a current insurance quote. Three blank rows close with the address; the fourth needs a broker.
  3. Get a broker opinion of value and an appraisal. The workbook cannot state a cap rate because no official source publishes one. This is the input that turns every scenario table on this site into a decision.
  4. Ask the tenant for unit-level sales and their renewal intention. A tenant that shares sales is signalling it wants the site. A refusal is also information.
  5. Line up a qualified intermediary before any listing. The 45-day identification and 180-day receipt deadlines are hard; missing them makes the sale taxable. S12 ↗
  6. Do not list until the appraisal and a replacement pipeline exist. Selling into a search, with a clock running, is how owners convert a good asset into a bad exchange.

6. What this workbook refuses to do

  • It will not tell you the property's value. Rent, basis, debt and the exact expiry were never provided.
  • It will not tell you your tax liability. Your marginal rates and depreciation history are withheld for privacy; the tax mechanics are modelled with stated assumptions so you can substitute your own numbers.
  • It will not quote a cap rate as fact. No official source publishes one, so the model runs a band and says so.
  • It will not publish a street address, parcel number or any personal identifier. The site is built to fail if one appears.

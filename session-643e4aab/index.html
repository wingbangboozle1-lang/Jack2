Sell the lease, or keep the rent?

A source-verified decision workbook for a single-tenant Jack in the Box on a triple-net lease in Central Los Angeles, expiry window 2030-2035.

Short answer. The rent is the asset, and the lease is what the rent is worth. Financially, holding to expiry wins in 67.2% of 10,000 simulated futures, but only because the model assumes the tenant renews at market. The decision that actually controls the outcome is whether the owner has a long, priced term to sell - not whether the corner is gentrifying. Do not hold passively to expiry. Either sign a long extension now and sell into it, or run a 1031 sale while at least five years of term remain. Read the executive summary →

The four decisions, on one clock

Each strategy is valued in nominal 2040 dollars per $100,000 of current NOI. Both the subject and any replacement asset are held as property: growing rent, reinvested cash, and a sale at the horizon net of costs and tax. A tactical note on method is in Verification (item V18) - the first version of this model compared holding against cash invested at a flat yield, which mechanically favoured holding, and it was discarded.

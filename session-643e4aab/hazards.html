Hazards and insurance

Earthquake, wildfire, flood and insurance-market risk, and what each black swan does to each decision.

The brief asks for black-swan events - the ones that could destroy the local economy. This is the page for them. Earthquake and wildfire are the two that matter in Los Angeles, and they are not modelled as "a bad year": they are the events that can remove the building, the tenant, or the insurance market.

Earthquake

The USGS Uniform California Earthquake Rupture Forecast puts the Los Angeles region at a 60% probability of an magnitude 6.7 or greater earthquake within 30 years, 46% for magnitude 7.0 and 31% for magnitude 7.5 S24 ↗. Those are 30-year figures; over the roughly four to nine years remaining on this lease, the probability is proportionally lower but not small. The exposure is not uniform across buildings - it depends on construction, age, soil and retrofit status, none of which were provided for this parcel.

The thing that actually hurts. Earthquake insurance typically carries a percentage deductible (commonly 10-15% of the limit). On a building where the deductible is 15% of a $1.3m structure, the deductible alone is about $200,000 of uninsured loss before a single dollar of coverage responds. In an NNN lease the tenant may carry the policy, but the owner still owns the uninsured gap and the re-leasing problem after the event. Confirm the deductible before extending.

Wildfire

The January 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires burned 37,469 acres, destroyed 16,251 structures, damaged 2,047 more and killed 31 people. The County estimated $5.2-10.1bn of lost economic output, $2.2-4.2bn of lost labour income, $900m-1.6bn of lost tax revenue, and about 6,800 businesses impacted S25 ↗. Neither fire reached the Central Los Angeles commercial corridor, but the second-order effects were regional and immediate: the insurance market repriced, and the FAIR Plan needed a $1bn assessment from its member insurers - the first such assessment in over 30 years S26 ↗.

The parcel's fire hazard class is genuinely unknown. CAL FIRE maps State Responsibility Areas; a densely built Los Angeles commercial parcel is a Local Responsibility Area, and the Office of the State Fire Marshal states plainly that Local Responsibility Area zones are adopted by the local jurisdiction S27 ↗. So the answer for this parcel comes from the City of Los Angeles, by address, and the address is withheld here by policy. The row is blank rather than guessed.

Flood and seismic site conditions

The parcel's FEMA flood zone requires the effective Flood Insurance Rate Map panel for the address S28 ↗; liquefaction and fault-rupture zones require a geotechnical screening by parcel. Both are blank for the same reason as the fire zone, and both can change insurance cost and lender appetite.

What a black swan does to each decision

EventEffect on SELLEffect on HOLDEffect on EXTEND
M6.7+ earthquake
USGS 30-yr probability 60%
 Poor timing - a buyer will price the event and lenders will re-underwrite. But if the building is undamaged, a sale converts an uninsurable exposure into cash. The worst position: you own the physical asset, the deductible and the downtime. Recovery takes years and rent may abate. Extension does not remove the physical exposure; it only guarantees the rent continues if the building survives.
Wildfire in the trade area
Two events in Jan 2025
 Sale is still possible - the asset is outside the burn scar - but buyers re-price regional insurance. Demand disruption and labour displacement can hit unit sales for a quarter or more, even without damage. Neutral on physical risk; positive if the tenant's own performance is resilient.
Insurance withdrawal
$1bn FAIR Plan assessment
 Sale becomes difficult exactly when you most want liquidity - no insurance means no lender, no lender means no buyer. You may end up in the FAIR Plan at a much higher premium with narrower terms, eating the cash flow. Extension locks the tenant into the site, but the tenant, not the owner, usually carries the insurance in an NNN lease - confirm that obligation survives a market withdrawal.
Tenant insolvency or a closure programme Selling is far harder with a dark building; the value drops toward land value plus demolition. The dominant, non-tail risk. A purpose-built QSR box is a specialised improvement. An extension signed now is a claim on a tenant that may not be able to honour it. Get a guaranty or a termination payment schedule.
The conclusion the hazard data drives. Physical risk cannot be diversified away inside a single parcel, and it is not priced into a cap-rate conversation. That is the strongest non-financial argument for a 1031 exchange into multiple properties or a diversified fractional interest: it does not reduce the regional hazard, it reduces the share of your wealth exposed to any single building. The tax deferral makes that diversification close to costless, which is the one place where the tax code and the risk analysis point in the same direction.

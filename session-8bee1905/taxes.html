Transaction costs

The full cost stack of a sale, with the official rate and source for every component, and the thresholds that matter more than the rates.

California is the expensive part. California has no preferential capital gains rate: the gain is taxed as ordinary income, at a top marginal rate of 12.30%. On top of that sits federal recapture at up to 25%, federal capital gain at up to 20%, the 3.8% net investment income tax where it applies, and — inside the City of Los Angeles above $5.4M — a 4% or 5.5% special transfer tax. Why the thresholds matter more than the rates

The Measure ULA cliff

For closings after 2026-06-30, the special tax is 4% of the whole consideration above $5.4M and below $10.9M, and 5.5% at or above $10.9M. It is not a bracket on the excess; once the threshold is crossed the rate applies to the entire consideration. A price negotiated just over a threshold can therefore net less than a price just under it. S04

The Proposition 13 floor

A sale resets the base-year value to market value, and the 1% levy with its 2% annual cap runs from that new base. A buyer prices the higher tax floor into the offer. Holding preserves the old base, which is a genuine but illiquid benefit that is lost the moment the deed records. S11 S12

Recapture does not defer with the rest

Depreciation already taken is recaptured at up to 25% federally. In an exchange that gain is deferred along with the rest, but it is not eliminated: the deferred recapture follows into the replacement property and is recognised eventually. A 1031 is a postponement, never a forgiveness. S09 S08

Two published county rates

The Registrar-Recorder page states the county documentary transfer tax as $0.55 per $500 and, on the same page, states that for the five special cities including Los Angeles the county rate is charged at the full $1.10 per $1,000. Both statements are official. This workbook uses the higher figure in the cost stack and flags the ambiguity rather than quietly choosing the cheaper reading. S05

What is deliberately blank

Brokerage commission and closing costs have no official published rate, so that line is blank. Clicking a generic percentage onto it would be an invented number in a table of sourced ones. Obtain a real listing agreement before costing a sale.

Overview

An anonymised, source-linked decision workbook for a Central Los Angeles Jack in the Box NNN investment with a 2030-2035 lease expiry.

The short answer. Fix the lease before pricing the building, and settle the jurisdiction before costing the sale. Extending on market terms and then selling with long remaining term improves the outcome whether the rent market turns out strong or weak, and it does not foreclose holding. Selling first, without that step, converts a repricing problem into a tax problem.

That is a decision rule, not a price. Rent, exact expiry, option structure, guaranty, tax basis and debt are not public, and this workbook publishes them as blanks rather than guesses.

The four questions that decide the outcome

1. Is the building inside the City of Los Angeles?

A sale above $5.4M inside the city carries an extra 4% transfer tax, and 5.5% at or above $10.9M, on top of the city base rate and the county tax. Outside the city that line disappears. It is a yes/no question worth six figures, and it is not answered in the brief. S04

2. Who actually owes the rent?

The operating entity is not disclosed. The franchisor's own balance sheet shows $1.43 billion of long-term debt and negative book equity of $901 million, and it is not the obligor on this lease unless it guaranteed it. S18

3. What does the sale actually net?

California taxes capital gain as ordinary income at up to 12.30%, with no preferential rate, on top of federal capital gain, recapture and the net investment income tax. The headline price is not the number that competes with holding. S07

4. What can one building lose?

The Los Angeles region has a 60% chance of an M6.7 or greater earthquake within 30 years and a 31% chance of M7.5. A single site cannot diversify that. This is the strongest argument for eventually exchanging into more than one asset. S13

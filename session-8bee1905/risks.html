Risk register

Every decision pathway, split into business-as-usual, structural and black-swan classes, each with a mechanism and a mitigation.

How to read the scores. Likelihood and impact are this session's qualitative judgement on a 1–5 scale, labelled as judgement. They are not published probabilities, and no official source assigns them. Where an official probability does exist — the earthquake figures — it is quoted in full rather than being replaced by a score.

22 pathways: 10 business-as-usual, 8 structural, 4 rare or black swan.

R01Renewal rent resets below the current contract rentBusiness-as-usual3/54/5Extend and hold; hold without extension; extend then sellAt expiry the tenant is not obliged to pay the old rent. The franchisor states it renews leases at then-current market rates. If the site's sales have not grown with rents, the renewal conversation is a rent cut, not a rent increase.Negotiate the extension two to three years before expiry, from a position where the sale alternative is priced. R02Tenant vacates at expiry and the site sits vacantBusiness-as-usual2/55/5Extend and hold; hold without extensionA single-tenant building has no other income. Vacancy means zero rent, immediate property tax and insurance carry, and a re-letting cost for a building purpose-built for one brand.Verify at the outset whether the renewal option is unilateral to the tenant or mutual, and whether there is a guaranty. Keep a reserve equal to at least two years of carrying cost. Marketing the asset while the lease still has term is the practical hedge.S17
R03Franchisee (not franchisor) credit failureBusiness-as-usual3/54/5All hold and extend branchesThe rent is owed by the operating entity that holds the lease, which may be a franchisee with a handful of stores. The public parent's balance sheet does not answer this question at all, and the parent is not the obligor unless it guaranteed.Obtain the tenant entity's financial statements, the guaranty, and 24 months of payment history. Do not credit-score this asset from the franchisor's 10-Q.S17
R04Cap-rate expansion compresses sale value before expiryStructural3/54/5Sell before expiry; extend then sellNet-lease pricing moves with long rates and with risk appetite. The 10-year Treasury was 4.88% and the 30-year 5.26% on the most recent print read. Every 50 bp of cap-rate expansion removes roughly 7% of value at a 7% cap.Price the asset against the current Treasury curve rather than a historical cap rate. Treat the sale decision as a dated decision, not an open option, because optionality in a rising-rate regime has a cost.S03
R05Short remaining term shrinks the buyer poolStructural4/53/5Hold without extension; sell close to expiryAs the lease runs down, buyers price rollover risk and lenders shorten amortisation or decline. The same NOI is worth less with three years of term than with twelve.Do not let the asset drift inside roughly five years of remaining term without a signed extension. The value of the extension is often greater than the cost of the rent concession that buys it.S03
R06Measure ULA transfer tax at saleBusiness-as-usual3/54/5Every sale branchFor closings after 2026-06-30 inside the City of Los Angeles, a sale above $5.4M and below $10.9M carries an additional 4% ULA tax on the consideration, on top of the 0.45% city base and the county documentary transfer tax. A sale at or above $10.9M carries 5.5%.Establish jurisdiction before pricing: unincorporated county area, or a different city, changes this completely. Model the sale net of ULA rather than gross. Consider whether a price just under $5.4M changes the net more than a higher headline.S04
R07Property tax reassessment on sale (Proposition 13)Business-as-usual5/53/5Every sale branch, through priceA change of ownership resets the base-year value to market value, with the 1% levy and a 2% annual cap running from the new base. A long-held asset with a low basis can see its tax bill multiply, which the buyer prices into the offer.Obtain the current assessed value and the annual tax bill. A sale hands the buyer a higher tax floor; that is already in the price. Holding preserves the low basis, which is a real but illiquid benefit.S11
R08Federal and state tax on gain, including recaptureBusiness-as-usual5/55/5Sell outright; sell and hold reservesDepreciation taken over the hold is recaptured at up to 25% federally. Remaining gain is taxed at up to 20% federally, plus 3.8% net investment income tax where it applies, plus California tax at ordinary rates up to 12.30% with no preferential capital gains rate. The state layer alone can exceed the entire federal capital gains rate.Compute the after-tax number from the actual basis and depreciation schedule before comparing strategies. Section 1031 defers the federal and state gain if the 45/180-day rules and qualified intermediary requirements are met. A sale cannot be modelled off a price alone.S09
R09Section 1031 execution failureBusiness-as-usual2/54/5Sell and exchange into diversified real propertyThe 45-day identification and 180-day completion windows cannot be extended for hardship outside a presidentially declared disaster. Taking control of proceeds disqualifies the exchange and makes the entire gain taxable in that year. Intermediary insolvency has caused exactly this.Use a qualified intermediary with segregated, insured accounts and check its bonding. Identify more than one replacement property under the rules. Have the replacement identified before the relinquished sale closes.S08
R10Reinvestment risk after a saleBusiness-as-usual4/53/5Sell and hold reserves; sell and exchangeCash released by a sale has to go somewhere. The 10-year Treasury at 4.88% is a benchmark, not an achievable net-lease yield on a comparable asset, and it carries no depreciation shelter, no rent growth and no inflation linkage.Set the hurdle before selling: the replacement yield net of costs and management must beat the retained rent, and the after-tax comparison must be made on a like-for-like basis. Do not compare a gross cap rate to a Treasury yield.S03

R15Population and employment base erosion in the trade areaStructural3/53/5Extend and hold; renewal rentLos Angeles County lost 3.2% of its population between the 2020 base and July 2025, and the city lost 0.8%, while metro payrolls grew 0.5%. Growth is narrow: education and health added 49,100 jobs while information lost 11,400 and financial activities lost 5,100. A restaurant's trade area is people and paycheques.Read this as a rent-growth constraint, not a closure signal. R16Cost of labour and food inputs squeezing the tenantBusiness-as-usual4/53/5Renewal rent; tenant survivalLocal food CPI rose 2.8% and the employment cost index 3.9% over twelve months, both above the national figures in the same release, while average hourly wages in the metro are $37.15. For a quick-service operator these are the two largest controllable cost lines, and they determine how much rent the store can carry.Ask for store-level P&L and rent coverage rather than relying on system averages. A tenant whose margin is compressed will seek a rent reduction at renewal regardless of the landlord's basis.S02
R17Interest-rate shock raising the cost of both holding and transactingStructural3/54/5Sell before expiry; refinance; exchangeHigher long rates lower the present value of a fixed rent stream and raise the cost of any replacement financing. The 30-year at 5.26% against a 10-year at 4.88% is a steep curve: term is being paid for.Fix the decision date rather than waiting. If refinancing, match tenor to the lease rather than to the market's convenience.S03

R20Concentration: one building, one tenant, one cityStructural5/54/5All hold branches, as a portfolio questionEvery risk above lands on the same balance sheet at the same time. The expected return can be attractive while the distribution is unacceptable, because a single event removes both the income and the collateral.This is the strongest structural argument for eventually exchanging into several properties or several tenants, and it is independent of the sell-versus-renew rent question. The correct sequencing is to fix the lease first so the asset prices well, then diversify.S13

The asset is illiquid and the buyer pool is thin.Match any loan maturity to the decision window, keep the entity documents current, and decide the succession question before the lease forces it. Forced sales are how good assets get bad prices.flagged

The black swans, separately

These are the events that do not show up in an expected-value calculation and would remove both the income and the collateral at once. The point of listing them apart is that the appropriate response is structural — diversification and reserves — not a price adjustment.

Earthquake damage or business interruption Rare / black swan

Mechanism: The Los Angeles region has a 60% chance of an M6.7 or greater earthquake within 30 years, 46% for M7.0 and 31% for M7.5. A single building is either damaged or it is not: the regional probability is not a diversification argument at the parcel level.

Mitigation: Obtain the seismic retrofit status and the ALQUIST-PRIOLO / seismic hazard zone designation for the parcel. Confirm earthquake coverage and deductibles, and whether business interruption is included. A single-asset owner carries this undiversified; an exchange into several assets in different regions spreads it.

Correlation is the real risk

Each row above is manageable on its own. The danger is that they arrive together, on one building, for one tenant, in one city, with no second asset to absorb the loss. That is why the recommendation treats diversification as a structural objective rather than a preference, and why extending before selling is the move that makes diversification affordable. S13

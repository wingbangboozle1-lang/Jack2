Area economy

Population, income, jobs, wages, prices and consumer demand, verified line by line against official sources.

Every row below was read in the cited page on 2026-11-10 and the locator column says where in that page the figure appears, so it can be re-checked in seconds. No row is filled in from another workbook, and no row is estimated. City, county and metro figures are proxies for the store's trade area, because the store's own catchment cannot be drawn without an address.

The mixed picture, stated plainly

Population is falling, income is holding

Los Angeles County lost 3.2% of its population between the April 2020 base and July 2025, and the city lost 0.8%. Median household income is $90,112 county-wide and $81,939 in the city, with poverty at 13.3% and 16.5% respectively. A shrinking base with static incomes is a rent-growth constraint, not a demand collapse. S01

Job growth is real but narrow

Metro payrolls grew 0.5% year over year to 6.28 million, with unemployment at 4.5% in the county. Almost all of the growth is education and health, which added 49,100 jobs. Information lost 11,400 and financial activities 5,100. Narrow growth is fragile growth. S02

Costs are rising faster than the nation

Local food CPI is up 2.8% against 2.5% nationally, and the local employment cost index is up 3.9% against 3.1% nationally. For a quick-service tenant these are the two largest controllable cost lines, and they set how much rent the store can carry at renewal. S02

The restaurant market is large and 2022-dated

City food-services sales were $17.37 billion and county sales $44.00 billion in the 2022 Economic Census. That describes the size of the local market. It is not evidence about this store's sales, which are private. S01

Note on schools

The brief asks about school systems as a value driver. This workbook does not publish a school-quality claim for the property, because attendance areas are assigned per parcel and that location is withheld. A district-level statistic attached to an unknown school would be exactly the kind of inference this brief forbids. What can be said is mechanical: in a quick-service trade area, schools matter through daytime population and family density, not through district test scores. If a school figure is needed, it should be pulled for the actual parcel's attendance area once the location is available for internal use.

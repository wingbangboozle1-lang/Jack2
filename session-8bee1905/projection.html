Projection limits

What can and cannot be projected honestly for this asset, and the arithmetic that shows why timing dominates forecasting.

This workbook does not publish a rent or price forecast. The brief asks for a reliable projection system. The reliable answer is a framework with explicit inputs and explicit limits, plus only official published projections with their vintages attached. A rent forecast built from eight annual observations would be false precision, and it would be the most confident-looking and least defensible thing on the site.

What is verified as an official projection

The California Department of Finance publishes county population and household projections. The page and the vintage are verified: the 2024 baseline, Vintage 2026, released 2026-03-13, with Report P-2A for county totals and P-4 for households. S16

What is not published here: the individual projected values. They are distributed as spreadsheet workbooks that this session's tooling cannot parse cell by cell, and copying a projected year out of another workbook would be precisely the unverified transcription this brief forbids. The projection series is therefore cited, and the numbers are left out.

Population is contracting while payrolls grow slowly and narrowly. For a landlord the practical reading is that rent growth in real terms should not be assumed. It is a constraint on assumptions, not a forecast.

A rate framework, not a forecast

The one projection that can be made honestly is arithmetic rather than prophecy: what a change in the exit capitalisation rate does to value, holding income constant. At a 7.0% exit cap, each 50 basis point move is roughly 7% of value. This is why the timing of a decision matters more than a forecast of rents.

Pure arithmetic: value = income / cap rate. No cap rate is asserted for this asset, in either direction, because no official source publishes transaction cap rates. The table exists to show how much a rate change is worth, which is the argument for setting a dated decision rather than waiting.

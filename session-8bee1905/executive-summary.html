Executive summary

The recommendation, the reasoning behind it, the cost of selling line by line, and the order in which the missing inputs should be closed.

Recommendation. Run a dual track now, not in 2030. In parallel: (1) obtain a written extension proposal from the tenant on market terms, and (2) obtain at least one executable sale offer. Then choose on after-tax net proceeds and replacement quality, at a decision date you set in advance and put in writing.

If the extension can be had at or near market rent, take it and sell with long remaining term. That route improves the outcome in both the sell case and the hold case, because short remaining term is what costs money at a sale and rollover risk is what a buyer prices. If the tenant will not extend on acceptable terms, the answer becomes price discovery plus a section 1031 exchange, in that order.

Why this, and not "sell" or "renew"

The brief asks for one decision. The honest answer is that the decision is not yet decidable from public information, because the five inputs that control it are private: contract rent, exact expiry and option terms, the obligor's identity and guaranty, tax basis and depreciation taken, and any secured debt. Every workbook on this repository that publishes a confident dollar ranking has had to invent those inputs. This one refuses to, and instead publishes the rule that turns those inputs into an answer, in the order they become available.

Extend the lease, then sell with long remaining term

Viable when: The tenant will sign a market-supported extension on acceptable terms, and the added term raises the price by more than the rent concession costs.

Principal risk: Paying for term with rent you cannot recover, or discovering the tenant will not sign at any price you can accept.

Attacks the two things that most reduce price, namely short term and rollover risk, without a taxable event. A longer lease widens the buyer pool and improves financeability. Preserves the deferral option for later.

Sources: S17 S03 S04

Extend and hold for income

Viable when: Renewal rent clears the debt service and reserve with real coverage, the owner can absorb a long vacancy and an insurance shock, and the concentration is acceptable to the owner's overall balance sheet.

Principal risk: A correlated local event that removes income and collateral simultaneously, with no second asset to absorb it.

Continues undiversified exposure to one building, one tenant and one seismic region. Defers, rather than avoids, the eventual tax. Requires capital reserves for roof, structure and systems.

Sources: S13 S15 S11

25% federal recapture + 20% federal capital gain + 3.8% net investment income tax + 12.30% California, on the applicable components, is the ceiling the sources support (S09, S10, S06, S07). It is a ceiling from four separate rules, not a single published total, and it is not applied as a flat rate to the whole gain.

The Measure ULA line is why jurisdiction comes first. At the demonstration price the special tax is zero only because $4.0M sits below the $5.4M threshold that applies to closings after 2026-06-30. The honest limitations

  • No price, no rent, no cap rate. None exists in public data, and none is invented here. S17
  • No parcel hazard answer. The fire zone, flood zone and seismic zone are parcel lookups that need an address, which is withheld by design. S15
  • No forecast of rents or values. Eight annual observations cannot support one honestly. See projection limits.
  • Regional data is not trade-area data. City and county figures are proxies for the store's catchment, and are labelled as proxies throughout.
  • 15 items are published as blank. They are all on the Flagged for review page with what would close each one.

Internal decision support. Not an appraisal, a tax opinion, legal advice or an offer. No street address, parcel number, owner name, loan number or account appears anywhere on this site.

Decision analysis

Six decision branches, each with when it is viable, what it gains, what it costs, its principal risk and the sources behind it.

Decision rule: Fix the lease before pricing the asset, and settle jurisdiction before costing the sale.

Six branches are analysed below. Each one is a real strategy that a landlord would consider, and each is judged on what it does to tax, concentration and optionality rather than on a forecast that cannot be supported.

The gates, in order

The order matters. Closing gate 3 before gate 1 can cost six figures; closing gate 6 after gate 4 is how exchanges fail.

What not to do

Do not drift to expiry

Every month of remaining term is part of what a buyer pays for. Doing nothing is not neutral: it is a decision to sell later at a lower multiple, or to renew from a weaker position. The cost of drift is invisible until it is large. S03

Do not price the asset off a gross cap rate

A headline cap rate ignores the California ordinary-income treatment of the gain, recapture, net investment income tax and, inside the city, a 4% or 5.5% special transfer tax. The number that competes with holding is the after-tax, after-cost net. S07 S04

Do not credit-score the asset from the franchisor's 10-Q

The obligor is the entity that signed the lease. The public parent's negative book equity is a capital-structure fact about a different company. S18

Do not start a 1031 clock without an intermediary

The 45-day identification and 180-day completion limits cannot be extended for hardship outside a presidentially declared disaster, and taking control of proceeds disqualifies the exchange entirely. S08

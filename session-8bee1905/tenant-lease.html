Tenant &amp; lease

The franchisor's reported financial position, and the lease facts that decide the answer and are not public.

Two different credit questions, usually confused. The franchisor is a public company whose filings can be read. The franchisee that signed this lease is not public, and may have a handful of restaurants. The franchisor's numbers are published below for context, with an explicit warning attached: they are not a credit opinion on the entity that owes this rent.

What to request, in one list

  1. The lease, all amendments, and any side letters.
  2. Every option and notice provision, with a calendar worked backwards from the expiry date.
  3. The guaranty, including any cap, burn-off or expiration clause on it.
  4. Twenty-four months of rent payment history and any deferral or abatement correspondence. S17
  5. The operating entity's financial statements, or an operating statement for this store.
  6. Store-level sales for the trailing 24 months and the resulting rent coverage.

None of these can be obtained from a public source. That is why the recommendation takes the form of an ordered process rather than a single number.

Projection method that can be reliable without pretending to know the answer

  1. Define geography privately. Use address/APN to identify correct municipal boundaries, census tract/block group, site access and a defensible drive-time or trade-area definition; do not publish the coordinate.
  2. Collect a consistent time series. Record each official source's native geography, release date, vintage, estimate/error range and revision status. Use ACS margins of error and avoid mixing incompatible boundary years.
  3. Model causal channels, not just population growth. Tie local household/demographic indicators to store-level tenant sales (if contractually available), employment/commute, competition, access, zoning, rent and tenant credit. Keep correlation/context separate from causal evidence.
  4. Run paths and stress cases. Model contract rent, renewal rent/likelihood (unknown until supported), vacancy, landlord costs, cap rates, insurance, tax/transaction cost, debt and owner tax. Add joint shocks (recession plus insurance shock; earthquake plus downtime), not only independent hazard assumptions.
  5. Update and back-test. Re-estimate at each material release and annual lease milestone; compare prior projected values to observed actual outcomes. No validated probability model can be calibrated from the facts currently supplied.

Central Los Angeles Jack in the Box, net lease: sell before expiry or renew?

One asset. A lease expiring somewhere in 2030–2035. Fourteen workbooks have now been produced on this question in this repository, from overlapping and partly assumed inputs. This page tells you which one to read first, why, and what none of them can tell you.

Start here: session-313ba936 (Session M)

It is the only build in this repository whose source register records, for every source, the exact text that was on the page on the access date, so a reviewer can diff the workbook against the live page without re-deriving anything. It is also the only one that discloses its own corrected methodology errors.

The answer it reaches: negotiate an extension first, then price it. Holding and selling land within a few percent of each other on central tendency — so the decision turns on the rent and term you can negotiate and on how much concentrated tail risk you are willing to carry, not on a forecast. It publishes the three breakevens that decide it rather than asserting a value.

What the evidence says, in six lines

The county is shrinking, officially

Los Angeles County population is 9,853,435 in 2025 and the state projects 9,637,524 by 2040 — a decline of 215,911 people. Census already shows −3.2% since 2020.

Population & schools →

The tenant is contracting

Jack in the Box same-store sales were −1.1% in Q3 FY2026, 17 restaurants closed in the quarter, and the estate is 53 units smaller than a year ago under a named closure programme.

Tenant credit →

The benchmark is 6.8%

The most recent official filing from a major net-lease buyer reports a 6.8% initial weighted average cash yield on Q3 2026 acquisitions, against a 4.88% 10-year Treasury.

Valuation & capital →

The hazard is local

USGS puts the Los Angeles region at 60% for a M6.7+ earthquake in 30 years. The Eaton Fire burned 14,021 acres inside this county in January 2025 and destroyed 9,419 structures.

Risk register →

Selling is taxed, holding is capped

City transfer tax 0.45%, county 0.11%, and Measure ULA adds 4% above $5.4m. Holding instead keeps the Proposition 13 assessed-value cap at 2% a year, which a sale resets.

The two tax rules →

The gap is in the tail, not the mean

Holding wins the central case by about 3%, but its 5th-percentile outcome is materially worse than the diversified path. That asymmetry, not the average, is the argument for exchanging.

Thirteen earlier workbooks, preserved

Every prior session's build is still here, unmodified, at its own path. They remain useful as corroboration and as a record of how the analysis evolved, but a reader should know what they are: earlier passes with different access dates, different source sets and different assumed rents. Several present dollar-valued recommendations as if the rent and cap rate were known. They are not. Where two builds disagree, the disagreement is usually the assumption, not the data.

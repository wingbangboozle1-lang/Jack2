What the evidence says

1. The local economy is stable, not booming

Metro employment grew +0.6% year over year with 6,289.1k jobs, and unemployment fell from 5.8% to 4.8% — but the growth is concentrated in education/health (+42.7k) while information (−12.3k, −6.1%) and professional services (−9.2k) contract. S1 ↗

County population is projected to fall from 9.85M (2025) to 9.64M (2040) in the state's official series. S3 ↗

2. The tenant is restructuring, not disappearing

2,136 Jack in the Box restaurants, 93% franchised, but the system shrank by 55 units in FY2025; Del Taco is being sold for $115M, the dividend is gone, and ~$1.7B of securitized debt carries sales-based covenants. S9 ↗

Critically for you: the company says it has “generally been able to renew our restaurant leases as they expire at then-current market rates.” S9 ↗ The 2030–2035 expiry is a repricing event, not an automatic rollover.

3. The money decision is tighter than it looks

At the benchmark rent, selling now clears about $2,214,000 after costs. Holding and renewing produces $5,690,267 of 2040 wealth versus $5,937,091 if you sell and redeploy at the verified 7.3% net-lease yield. S10 ↗

You need a 6.97% reinvestment yield for selling to match holding. That is why the recommendation is a sequence, not a slogan.

4. The tail risk is real and documented

The January 2025 fires destroyed 16,251 structures, cost $5.2B–$10.1B of economic output, hit ~6,800 businesses and ~47,000 workers, and removed $900M–$1.6B of tax revenue. S13 ↗

Central Los Angeles flatland is not the wildland interface, but smoke, shut-offs, insurance repricing and seismic exposure are metro-wide and unhedged by a single-asset portfolio.

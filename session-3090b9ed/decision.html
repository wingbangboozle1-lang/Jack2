Breakevens (assumption basis: c0 6.5%, g 2%, w 6.5%)

If you can reinvest sale proceeds above 8.64% (assumption basis), selling now wins in every expiry year; if your exit cap would exceed the exit-cap breakeven for the realistic year, holding to that year loses. Both thresholds are assumption-dependent until the real rent, expiry and appraisal arrive.

The reinvest breakeven is identical across expiry years by algebra, not by error: with exit cap equal to going-in cap and identical transfer taxes, the (1-a^n) terms cancel and w* = (1+g)/a - 1 where a = (1-transfer)/(c0 + 1 - transfer). Independently re-derived and matched to six decimals.

Recommended course of action: DUAL TRACK, time-boxed: (1) open a confidential extension / blend-and-extend inquiry now targeting >=10 years of term at or above in-place rent with CPI-linked escalators and a strengthened guaranty; (2) in parallel, prepare a Section 1031 sale so the asset can be sold quickly if the extension fails or the appraisal clears the breakeven; (3) never let the asset drift into the final 3-4 years of term unmanaged.

Why: OPT-C has the highest modelled expectation (mean 41.46, best in 56.6% of trials); OPT-A has the best modelled tail (P05 27.98, sd 6.80) and is the hedge against tenant refusal and exit-cap risk; OPT-B and OPT-D are dominated or near-dominated. The dual track keeps both live, which is exactly what the fifteen prior workbooks independently converged on (see reconciliation.json).

Confidence: Moderate on the shape of the decision (sequence), low on any specific number until the owner facts (Q01-Q04) and parcel facts (Q05, Q08, Q09) arrive. All model outputs are assumption-driven (IR-14).

Balance of risk: Risk is managed by: time-boxing (T3, T6), keeping the sale option warm (dual track), confirming insurance/hazards before any long hold (T5), and diversifying a concentrated single-asset position through the 1031 branch (R17).

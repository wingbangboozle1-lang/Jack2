Decision strategies

HOLD - renew / extend the NNN lease to the 2030-2035 window and beyond analyzed

Objective: wealth preservation + stable income growth

Renewal keeps the asset's income stream and defers all transaction costs. It is the strongest option if the tenant's covenant holds and an early extension can be negotiated with CPI-linked escalators before the expiry window.

Pros

  • No transfer tax and no disposition costs
  • Preserves depreciated-basis tax shelter and Prop 13 low base (1% levy, M81)
  • Full control over timing: can still sell before expiry once renewal terms are known
  • CPI-linked escalators (metro CPI +3.7%, M96) can protect real income

Cons

  • Concentrated single-tenant credit: system SSS -1.1% and 50-60 planned closures in FY2026 (M112, M118)
  • Exposes the owner to earthquake and wildfire tail risks with unverified parcel insurance (M98-M111)
  • Re-tenanting at expiry is a real cost: the tenant is shrinking (R12)
  • Population and school-enrollment declines are structural headwinds (M06, M33)

Switch triggers

  • Renewal term sheet with escalator >= CPI floor secured by 2028
  • Tenant site-level sales and guaranty confirmed (Q05, Q13)
  • Insurance quote bound for the full renewal term (Q09)
  • If any trigger fails by 2028: switch to SELL

SELL - outright sale before the NNN expiry analyzed

Objective: liquidity and wealth reallocation; risk transfer

Selling converts the asset to cash now, transferring tenant and hazard risk. It is the cleanest wealth-preservation move if the parcel is in City limits (ULA cost known) and the bid clears the owner's reserve after tax.

Pros

  • Immediate liquidity; risk shifts to the buyer
  • Clean exit ahead of a shrinking tenant system and the 2030-2035 expiry
  • Establishes a price in the current market instead of betting on 2030-2035 conditions
  • Avoids any re-tenanting capital call

Cons

  • Transaction friction: County DTT 0.11% plus City base 0.45% and, if in City, ULA 4.45%/5.95% all-in (M77-M80) on a sale above $5.4M
  • Capital-gains tax up to 13.30% CA top marginal plus federal (M85) unless a 1031 exchange is used
  • Forces a reinvestment decision into a 7.3% net-lease benchmark market (M71)
  • Buying pool narrows near the ULA threshold (R13)

Switch triggers

  • Appraisal or broker opinion supports a price above the owner's reserve net of ULA and tax (Q06, Q16)
  • Jurisdiction confirmed so ULA is priced, not guessed (Q01)
  • If bids miss the reserve: fall back to D-HOLD with an early extension

EXCHANGE - sell now into an IRC Section 1031 like-kind exchange analyzed

Objective: growth with tax deferral; risk transfer

A 1031 exchange sells the Central LA single-tenant asset and reinvests into like-kind replacement real property, deferring gain. Official constraints: real property held for business or investment only (M86), 45-day identification and 180-day receipt deadlines (M87-M88), qualified intermediary required.

Pros

  • Defers capital-gains tax (up to 13.30% CA top marginal, M85) while redeploying equity
  • Allows diversification away from one tenant and one parcel's hazard exposure
  • Same transfer-tax cost as a cash sale, so the comparison is purely tax-driven
  • Reinvestment into the 7.3% net-lease benchmark (M71) or other income property is documented as available

Cons

  • Hard deadlines: 45 days to identify, 180 days to close (S12); failure recognizes gain
  • Exchange quality risk: buying illiquid replacement property to defer tax can destroy value
  • Still pays ULA/county transfer tax on the relinquished property if in City
  • Requires basis and loan data the owner must supply (Q07); 1031 applies to the real property only

Switch triggers

  • Qualified intermediary engaged before listing and replacement inventory pre-identified
  • Owner's tax counsel confirms the basis position (Q15, Q07)
  • If no acceptable replacement exists within the deadline: convert to D-SELL

RECOMMENDED COURSE OF ACTION - dual-track: negotiate an early extension while preparing sale pricing in parallel RECOMMENDED

Objective: balance of all three: preservation, growth, manageable risk

Do not choose between renew and sell yet: 22 blocking questions (Q01-Q22) and 26 blank parcel facts (F-N1 to F-N3) prevent an honest single-path answer. The best course of action today is (1) resolve jurisdiction, lease terms, hazard and insurance diligence immediately, (2) open extension talks with the tenant on CPI-linked terms, and (3) obtain an appraisal and test the market so a sell/1031 decision can be made on evidence by 2028, two years before the earliest expiry. The model publishes breakeven conditions for each path so the switch points are pre-agreed rather than emotional.

Pros

  • Keeps every option open while information gaps close (wealth preservation first)
  • Uses official current data (labor, rates, tenant filings) instead of stale forecasts
  • Pre-agreed triggers remove bias from the eventual decision
  • Extending first can raise rent even if the asset is later sold

Cons

  • Requires owner time and negotiation capital now
  • Appraisal and diligence costs are sunk even if the hold path wins

Switch triggers

  • Q01-Q05 answered within 30 days (identity, lease, tenant)
  • Q06-Q11 answered within 90 days (value, hazard, insurance)
  • Decision gate at 2028: renew if all R01-R03 mitigations are in place and appraisal supports value; otherwise sell or exchange

Assumptions (labeled, not facts)

  • base NOI growth g = 2.0%/yr — Below the LA metro CPI-U 3.7% (M96) and the LA-CSA ECI 3.9% (M97) because county population is declining (M06: -0.6%) and school enrollment falls (M33). Held flat in real terms is optimistic; 2.0% is a deliberately conservative nominal anchor.
  • base required return r = 7.0%/yr — 10-year Treasury 4.98% (M66) plus a ~200bp private-real-estate risk premium; swept 6.0-9.0% in the grid.
  • base going-in cap cap0 = 7.3% — The only official yield datapoint available: Realty Income Q2-2026 initial weighted-average cash yield of ~7.3% (M71). The subject asset's own cap rate is a blank (M145) - the grid sweeps 6.0-9.0%.
  • base exit cap drift = +10bp per year — Neutral-to-slightly-adverse: population decline and tenant shrinkage argue against cap-rate tightening; grid sweeps -20bp to +40bp per year.
  • hold horizon T swept 4-9 years — The stated NNN expiry window is 2030-2035; from 2026 that is 4 to 9 years.
  • transfer tax applied on the sale path only — County DTT 0.11% (M80); City all-in 4.45% tier-1 / 5.95% tier-2 (M78/M79) if the parcel is in City limits - both are modelled as separate cases because jurisdiction is a blank (M132).
Capital-gains tax on an outright sale is not rendered as a number because the owner's basis (M143) is a blank. The verified top marginal California rate is 13.30% (M85, source S31/S32). Obtain basis from tax records (Q07) before comparing after-tax outcomes.

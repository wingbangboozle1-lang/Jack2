Question. A single-tenant Jack in the Box restaurant in central Los Angeles is leased on net terms, and the lease expires at some point between 2030 and 2035. Should the owner sell before the lease expires, or renew it?

Short answer. Ask the tenant for an early extension first, then decide. A refusal points to selling before expiry; an agreement leaves a real choice between keeping a secured income and selling a more valuable lease.

The rule, by answer

  1. If the tenant refuses or stalls on an extension, then sell before expiry while term remains; exchange if the tax basis is low. Do not wait for expiry.
  2. If the tenant signs, and the owner can live with one tenant in one region, then keep the property. Revisit a sale later from a position of strength.
  3. If the tenant signs, but the owner wants preservation first, then sell the extended lease and exchange into a longer lease elsewhere.

Blank until the private file is checked

  • On what exact date does the lease expire? (Q01)
  • What is the rent today, and does it rise during the term? (Q02)
  • Does the tenant hold renewal options: how many, at what rent, with what notice window? (Q03)
  • Who is the tenant: the company, a subsidiary or a franchisee, and is there a guaranty? (Q04)
  • Is the lease fully net: who pays taxes, insurance, roof, structure and parking lot? (Q05)
  • What happens under the lease after a fire, earthquake or condemnation? (Q06)
  • Will the tenant sign an early extension if asked now? (Q09)
  • How is this restaurant trading: yearly sales and rent as a share of sales? (Q10)

First steps

  1. Weeks 1 to 2: Assemble the private file: lease and every amendment, rent ledger, property tax bill, insurance certificates, loan papers, title report.
  2. Weeks 1 to 2: Look the parcel up privately in the official tools: Assessor, zoning, fire, flood and earthquake-zone maps, cleanup database.

Executive Summary

Important details for the decision: whether to sell a Central Los Angeles Jack in the Box NNN property before its 2030–2035 lease expiry, or renew the lease based on the area's economic activity. Facts are sourced; the recommendation is clearly labeled analysis.

The situation

  • Asset: single-tenant Jack in the Box restaurant property in Central Los Angeles under a triple-net (NNN) lease expiring between 2030 and 2035 (exact year and rent are property-specific facts held privately and anonymized here — see Property).
  • Lease economics: per the tenant's own SEC disclosure, its restaurant leases "generally provide for fixed rental payments (with cost-of-living index adjustments) plus real estate taxes, insurance, and other expenses" — i.e., the owner carries an inflation-indexed, largely pass-through income stream (FY2025 10-K, Item 2).
  • Decision clock: the lease's remaining term (≈4–9 years) is the single largest value lever the owner controls through timing.

What the verified data says

  • Area demand is stable long-term. The adopted regional forecast projects Los Angeles County households +22.5% and employment +8.6% by 2050 (SCAG), while recent Census estimates show LA city population roughly flat (−0.8%, 2020–2025; Census). The metro labor market is solid: 4.4% unemployment and leisure & hospitality jobs growing +1.5% YoY (BLS).
  • Inflation favors the owner while the lease runs. LA CPI is +3.7% YoY (Sept 2026) and the lease's escalators are cost-of-living linked (BLS CPI; 10-K).
  • Tenant credit is the weak link. Jack in the Box Inc. posted FY2025 revenues of $1.465B (down two years running), a net loss of $80.7M, a stockholders' deficit of $938.3M, eliminated its dividend, sold Del Taco to repay debt, and is closing 150–200 underperforming restaurants under "JACK on Track" (SEC 10-K; company release). 93% of its restaurants are franchised, and its own risk factors warn of franchisee bankruptcies and closures.
  • Cost pressure on the restaurant. California's $20/hr fast-food minimum wage (AB 1228) drove ~7.6% wage inflation in the tenant's company restaurants in FY2025 (CA DIR; 10-K).
  • Tail risks are quantifiable and material. USGS puts a 60% probability on a M≥6.7 earthquake in the LA region within 30 years; the January 2025 Palisades and Eaton fires destroyed 16,264 structures and led to the first FAIR Plan insurer assessment in 30+ years (USGS; CAL FIRE; CA CDI).

The decision (analysis)

Recommended course of action: negotiate an early lease renewal/extension now — target ≥10 years with CPI-banded escalations — and then either hold the enhanced income stream or sell at a materially higher price thanks to the longer lease term. If a market-competitive renewal cannot be secured within the deadline window for the property's expiry year, sell while ≥5–7 years of term remain, structured as a §1031 exchange to defer federal and California tax on the gain.

  • Never hold passively through the 2030–2035 expiry without a renewal: that combines the worst pricing (short remaining term), vacancy/downtime risk, and a deteriorating-credit tenant backdrop.
  • Why not sell immediately? A sale now forfeits ~3%/yr CPI-linked escalations and any renewal-driven value uplift, and crystallizes tax friction (California taxes capital gains as ordinary income — FTB). But selling is the right branch if the owner's priorities are simplification, liquidity, or de-concentration from a single weakening tenant — the analysis is built to support either choice with the same facts.
  • Risk balance for an individual owner: the balanced profile is (i) extended lease term, (ii) an explicit earthquake-insurance decision, (iii) staged exit checkpoints, and (iv) no over-concentration in one tenant — achieved either by holding with a fresh long lease or by exchanging into a diversified asset.

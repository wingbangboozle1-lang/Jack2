Lease-relevant behavior (as disclosed by the tenant)

  • Lease structure: "Our restaurant leases generally provide for fixed rental payments (with cost-of-living index adjustments) plus real estate taxes, insurance, and other expenses. For Jack in the Box, approximately 13% of the leases provide for contingent rental payments between 1% and 10% of the restaurant's gross sales once certain thresholds are met." (10-K, Item 2)
  • Renewal record: "We have generally been able to renew our restaurant leases as they expire at then-current market rates." (10-K, Item 2)
  • Property interests: of 2,136 JIB restaurants — 164 on company-owned land; 565 company-owned buildings on leased land; 1,020 company-leased buildings on leased land; 387 franchise directly-owned/leased (10-K, Item 2). The company is itself a major net-lease lessee and subleases most franchised sites.
  • Risk disclosures the owner should price in: franchisee bankruptcies/restaurant closures and delayed payments; possible inability to renew leases "without substantial additional cost, if at all"; ~70% CA+TX concentration; labor-cost regulation (AB 1228 cited by name as an FY2025 impairment driver) (10-K, Item 1A).

Analysis Credit read-through: the tenant remains cash-generative ($162M operating cash flow FY2025) and is actively repairing its balance sheet (debt paydown, Del Taco sale, dividend elimination) — but the system is shrinking, same-store sales are negative, California wage regulation is squeezing unit economics, and the closure program means every lease's renewal cannot be assumed. This is why the decision framework makes early renewal — or an orderly exit with term remaining — the two primary paths.

Executive summary

Sell the asset, or renew the lease? Hold the option open, then price it.

Bottom line first, then the reasoning, then the three numbers that decide it.

Recommendation

Do not renew on autopilot and do not sell on fear. Run a sequenced plan over 90–365 days: (1) assemble the missing documents, (2) verify parcel hazards and insurance, (3) open an extension negotiation that trades term for a market-rent reset, (4) obtain an appraisal, and (5) sell into a 1031 exchange only if the implied cap rate is at or below 6.00% or the replacement yield clears the thresholds below. If the cap rate is above 7.00%, keep and extend.

Why this is the answer

The two paths are close, so the tie-breaker is the lease

Base case: holding produces $5,675,912 of 2040 wealth; selling now and redeploying at the verified 7.3% net-lease benchmark produces $5,937,091. The gap is about -$261,180 — roughly 4% of wealth, inside the error bars of the assumptions.

Selling beats holding in the bear case by $704,151; holding wins the bull case by $419,329. A coin-flip decision should not be made on a coin flip — it should be made on the one variable you control: the rent and term you can negotiate.

Only one action improves both outcomes

A market-rent reset needs just +7.5% to make holding beat selling into the net-lease benchmark, and it simultaneously raises the price a buyer will pay because the WALT extends.

The tenant has structural reason to deal: its franchise agreements run 20 years while this lease ends in 2030–2035. S9 ↗

Selling is defensible — if capital is redeployed

You need 6.96% on the proceeds to match holding. One net-lease REIT reported a 7.3% initial weighted average cash yield on 2025 acquisitions; that is a real benchmark, but it is not a guarantee for a small private buyer. S10 ↗

Section 1031 lets you defer the gain and diversify one parcel into several. S16 ↗ That is the strongest argument for selling — not the cap rate.

Renewing is defensible — if the rent resets

Holding keeps a 2% annual cap on assessed value in a metro where CPI is running 3.7%. S6 ↗ S1 ↗ That is a genuine, permanent, non-transferable benefit.

The cost of holding is concentration: one tenant, one parcel, one metro, with wildfire/earthquake/insurance tail risk that diversification would remove.

What to do in the next 90 days

  1. Pull twelve documents (P01–P12): lease and amendments, exact expiry and option terms, rent roll, expense history, basis and depreciation, loan documents, APN, property condition report, insurance declarations, franchisee financials, a broker opinion of value, and zoning.
  2. Close the hazard gaps: FEMA flood panel, CAL FIRE/LACoFD fire hazard severity zone, CGS seismic and liquefaction screening, and a current insurance quote. All four are currently unverified for the parcel.
  3. Open the tenant conversation with a specific ask: market reset with a floor, 10-year extension, guaranty, and clarity on the option notice date.
  4. Do not list until the appraisal is in hand and the replacement-property pipeline exists.

What this workbook will not do

  • It will not state the property's value. Rent, expiry date, basis and debt are not in the brief, so the model publishes a benchmark and breakevens instead (see Flagged for review).
  • It will not quote a capital-gains rate. That needs the owner's basis and depreciation history; a 1031 exchange is the mechanism that makes the timing decision matter. S16 ↗
  • It will not use a secondary or industry source for any value. Where no official source exists, the cell is blank.

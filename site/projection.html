3. How each branch is priced

All four decisions use the same formula. This is deliberate: if branches were priced on different bases, the comparison would be meaningless.

The valuation identity

A net-lease buyer does not pay rent / cap for a lease with four years left — that would overvalue a short lease badly. A buyer pays:

PV at the market cap of contract rent through expiry  +  PV at the market cap of the reversion, where the reversion is the box re-let at market rent.

That is implemented as market_price(). It is the reason "sell now" and "sell at expiry" differ by more than a discount factor: the sale at expiry carries a +150 bps roll premium on the cap rate because the buyer is buying a rolling lease, not a term lease.

A — Sell now

market_price(rent, remaining_term, escalator, cap_today) × (1 − 2.0% selling cost). No further rent. The 2.0% transaction cost is a convention, not a sourced figure.

B — Hold to lease expiry

Contract rent for the remaining term, then a sale at cap_today + 1.50% applied to a probability-weighted exit rent that blends renew-at-market with re-let-after-downtime. A downtime cost of roughly ten months of rent is charged if the tenant does not renew.

C — Renew and hold

The roll happens inside your ownership instead of at the exit. Rent is blended at the roll, grows at the escalator, and the asset is sold at year 10 at the stabilised cap (no roll premium, because the renewed lease carries term).

D — Extend early, sell a longer-WALT asset

Give up 2.0% of rent immediately, add five years of term, sell at the stabilised cap instead of cap + 150 bps. The concession and the extension length are policy choices, disclosed as such.

4. The black-swan overlay

Drawn independently per trial at 1.65% frequency (66 hits in 4,000 trials), with severity drawn uniformly:

  • 35–85% of rent lost for a period (6–18 months of interruption)
  • 20–45% permanent loss of exit value

The asymmetry is the whole point. A sale completed before the event is insulated: branch A is left untouched. A held asset takes the full hit. That is not a modelling trick, it is what fire, earthquake, flood and tenant insolvency actually do — they arrive without warning and they arrive while you own the thing.

An honest limitation

The 2.0% annual event probability is a modelling assumption, not a sourced actuarial rate. It was chosen to sit in a defensible range for a ten-year hold on an urban Los Angeles infill parcel exposed to seismic, wildfire-smoke, flood and tenant-credit shocks. The conclusion is robust to it: rerunning at 0.5% or 6.0% changes the medians but not the ranking. That robustness is what makes the recommendation usable.

6. Reproducibility

This output is verifiable

python3 model/project_model.py --check regenerates the report and compares it to the checked-in data/model_output.json. Any drift fails loudly. The random generator is the stdlib Mersenne Twister under a fixed seed, with an explicit Box-Muller transform so the result does not depend on the interpreter's random.gauss. The site is generated from the same JSON by model/build_site.py, so no figure on this site is typed by hand.

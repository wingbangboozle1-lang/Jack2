Decision analysis

Sell now, hold to expiry, renew, or extend early — and the rule that separates them

Four options, one valuation basis. The recommendation is expressed as a decision rule with a computable threshold, so it stays correct when your own required return or the market cap rate moves away from the base case.

Model: model/project_model.py · seed 20261106 · all four branches discounted at the same rate

The decision rule

Hold and extend when your required return is below the crossover. Sell when it is above.

At a 7.10% cap rate and six years of term, the crossover is 7.39%. At 7.85% it is 8.15%. At 9.35% it is 9.67%.

The intuition is old and simple: if the market capitalises this cash flow more expensively than you do, you own the asset. If you capitalise it more expensively than the market, the market owns it — and you should let it.

1. The four options

A · LIQUIDATE

Sell now

$3,265,780
median PV · P(best) 1.7%

Banks the buyer's capitalisation of your contract rent plus the reversion. Removes roll risk, tenant concentration and every black swan at once. Costs you the upside if the tenant renews and cap rates stay put. Triggers a Prop 13 reset for the buyer's benefit, not yours.

B · DO NOTHING

Hold to expiry

$2,995,747
median PV · P(best) 0.0%

The dominated branch. You carry the roll and then sell a short-WALT asset into a wider cap. P(best) is effectively zero in every scenario tested. Included for completeness because it is the default if no one decides.

C · RENEW

Renew and hold 10 years

$3,697,184
median PV · P(best) 98.3%

Highest median because you keep the asset through the roll instead of selling into it. Conditioned on your required return being below the crossover. Adds concentration risk on a tenant whose same-store sales are falling.

D · EXTEND

Extend early, sell long WALT

$3,338,050
median PV · P(best) 0.0%

The practical compromise, and the one a broker will propose. Concede ~2% of rent now, buy five years of term, sell at the stabilised cap. Lower median than C but with a firm exit date you control.

Read it as a single rule: when your required return is above the cap rate (negative spread), selling wins. When it is below, holding wins. The crossover sits roughly 30–40 bps above the cap rate at six years of term, because holding also forecloses the option to sell into today's bid.

Interpretation: with four years left you would need the market to reprice to 10.87% before "sell now" beats "keep the rent and sell at expiry". With nine years left it takes only 8.51%. Short remaining term is, counter-intuitively, a reason to hold to expiry rather than dump the asset, because there is little term left to lose and you keep the rent.

7. The option you should not overlook

Sell at expiry is dominated — and it is the most likely default

Branch B is the outcome if no decision is made: you collect contract rent until the lease runs out, then sell whatever is left. You have carried every risk the owner faces and then sell the asset at the moment it is worth least relative to its stabilised value — into a market that is pricing the roll.

If your required return is above the crossover, sell now, not at expiry. If it is below, renew. There is almost no coherent position in between.

8. What would change this answer

  • You discover the tenant is a thinly capitalised single-unit franchisee with no guaranty. That widens the cap spread and pushes the crossover down toward "sell".
  • The unit's sales are weak. If rent is above ~10–12% of unit sales, the tenant cannot afford a market renewal and the box is not relettable to another QSR at that rent. That is the strongest argument for selling.
  • Your loan has a punitive prepayment penalty. If retiring the debt costs more than the spread you are capturing, the sale is not executable and the rule is moot.
  • You need liquidity or have a concentration limit. Then the decision is not an NPV comparison at all. Sell, and say so out loud rather than reverse-engineering a cap rate.
  • A genuine cap-rate dislocation. The breakeven table above tells you how wide it has to be.

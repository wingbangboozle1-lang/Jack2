Executive summary

The decision, six findings with their sources, a dated plan, and the conditions that would reverse it.

The answer in one page

Do not hold to expiry and do not simply renew. Run a single track: ask for an extension now at or above in-place rent, and if it is granted, sell in 2032 with roughly five to nine years of term left, through a §1031 exchange. If the tenant will not extend on acceptable terms, sell now through the same exchange. The difference between those two paths is 2.2% of certainty-equivalent wealth — small enough that the negotiation outcome, not the model, decides. What is not close: holding passively to 2030–2035 sits 5.7% below the leader on the risk measure, with a 5th-percentile outcome $176,615 worse per $100,000 of NOI.

What would change the answer

  • Rent growth at 2.0% or below makes sell-now first in the sensitivity grid: with weak growth the extra term is not worth waiting for.
  • A replacement asset that grows 3.0% also flips the ranking to sell-now.
  • An extension at a materially lower rent destroys the case for extending; sell into the existing income instead.
  • Insurance costs above ~10% of NOI would make the hold case uneconomic regardless of the lease.
  • A tenant default or a bankruptcy filing removes the choice entirely: the box is sold vacant at land-and-improvements value.

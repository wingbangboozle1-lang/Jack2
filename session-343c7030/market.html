What this means for the property

  • The trade area is dense and established, not growing. 8,304 residents per square mile in the city (D18) is a demand base a suburban site cannot match; −0.8% city and −3.2% county population change (D02, D04) means a buyer will not pay for growth.
  • Income supports value menus, not premium ones. A city median of $81,939 with 16.5% poverty (D05, D09) is a value-oriented QSR catchment — consistent with the tenant's format, and a reason the unit's sales are sensitive to local employment.
  • Rents and home values are high, which caps disposable income. $1,933 median gross rent and $921,200 median home value (D12, D11) leave less cash for away-from-home food than the same income elsewhere.
  • The school district is large and stable. 497,105 students in LAUSD for 2025-26 (K01) is a demand anchor for family households, but a district of that size says nothing about a specific site.

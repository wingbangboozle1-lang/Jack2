Projection model

Five strategies, 2026–2040, deterministic and seeded Monte-Carlo, with every assumption published and stress-tested.

What is modelled. Five courses of action, each projected from 2026 to 2040 on the same random paths, then ranked on certainty-equivalent wealth — the risk-free amount that a risk-averse owner would accept in place of the uncertain distribution — with a CRRA coefficient of 2.5. Expected value alone would reward the strategy with the fattest tail; the certainty equivalent does not.

The formulas

NOI(t)        = 100,000 × (1 + g)^(t − 2026)
cap(t)        = cap₀ + term adjustment (0–100 bp depending on years of term remaining)
price(t)      = NOI(t) ÷ cap(t)
city tax      = 0.45% × price + Measure ULA (4% if price > $5.4m, 5.5% if ≥ $10.9m)
net proceeds  = price − 2.5% selling costs − city tax − income tax (0 if §1031 is used)
terminal      = net proceeds × (1 + reinvestment growth)^(2040 − sale year), then taxed on exit
utility       = W^(1−γ)/(1−γ);  CE = [ mean(utility) ]^(1/(1−γ))
hazard        = annual quake probability derived from the verified 30-year 60% (1 − 0.40^(1/30))

Assumptions

Every number below is a modelling choice, not an official statistic. Each is shown with the range that was tested. The verified inputs the model does use are the September 2026 LA CPI (3.7%), the 10-year Treasury (4.86%), the earthquake probability (60% / 30 years) and the Measure ULA schedule.

Moving the expiry from 2030 to 2035 changes the hold outcome by $33,318 — 2.0% — for a fact the owner already knows and a buyer cannot see. Fixing it in a lease document is worth more than a year of rent escalation.

Limitations

  • No rent, address, size, basis or loan terms were supplied, so this measures a decision, not a parcel.
  • The cap rate is an assumption. If the asset trades at 7.0% rather than 6.0%, every dollar figure falls by about 14%.
  • The hazard module uses the verified probability of a major earthquake but an assumed loss given event (A13).
  • The owner's own tax position (bracket, state, entity, depreciation history) is deliberately not collected; flat top rates are applied.
  • No forecast of a recession, an earthquake or a wildfire is made. They are sampled as risks, not predicted.

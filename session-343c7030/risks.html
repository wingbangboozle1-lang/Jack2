Risks

18 business-as-usual and 12 black-swan events, each with its mechanism, its official anchor (or the absence of one), and its mitigation.

How to read the register. Each risk states what it does to value, whether an official source supports its probability, and what to do about it. Where no official probability exists — which is most of the time — the row says so rather than inventing one. Severity is the modelled impact on the decision, not a claim of actuarial precision.

How the tail enters the model

The Monte Carlo does not forecast disasters; it samples them. The annual probability of a major earthquake is derived from the verified 30-year figure (1 − 0.401/30 ≈ 3.0%), and each event applies an assumed retained loss (A13). Wildfire is modelled at 0.5% a year because the parcel's fire zone is unknown (H05). Replacing those two assumptions with the parcel's actual FHSZ class and insurance terms is the single most valuable piece of additional work available.

The three that should decide the question

  1. Tenant non-renewal (RK01). The owner's entire optionality depends on a counterparty whose equity is negative. A drive-through box is not easily re-let, and the drive-through entitlement is the most valuable and least transferable part of it.
  2. Earthquake (RK19). A 60% probability over 30 years is not a tail in the colloquial sense; it is a coin flip weighted twice. It is also the risk most likely to arrive at the same moment as a liquidity freeze.
  3. Insurance (RK08, H07). The largest genuinely unknown number in the file. A premium above 10% of NOI changes the hold case from marginal to uneconomic, and no official series publishes it for a private parcel.

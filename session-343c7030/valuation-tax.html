Valuation & tax

Cap rates, the Treasury curve, the Measure ULA cliff, Proposition 13 and §1031 — the mechanics that decide the net proceeds.

How a net-lease asset is priced. Value = net operating income ÷ capitalisation rate. Nothing else. Rent growth moves the numerator; interest rates and tenant credit move the denominator. Almost every dispute about "what it is worth" is a dispute about the cap rate.

A sale at $5,399,999 pays $24,300 of city tax. A sale at $5,400,001 pays $242,700. One dollar of price costs $218,400. Above $10.9m the same cliff repeats at 5.95%. If the asset is worth anywhere near a threshold, the pricing decision matters more than a year of rent.

§1031 in one paragraph

Gain is deferred, not forgiven. Within 45 days of closing you must identify replacement property in writing, and within 180 days you must close; the old basis carries into the new property (X09, X10, S11). Because the basis carries, the deferral compounds: that is why S1 and S3 beat S2 in every run of the model, by 4.4% of certainty-equivalent wealth.

Interpretation - what this means for the decision

Demand side: adequate, softening at the margin

City population is flat to slightly down (−0.8% since 2020) and the county is projected to decline to 9.64M by 2040. S2 ↗ S3 ↗ Households still earn a median $81,939 and spend $11,858 a year on food. S2 ↗ S1 ↗ A drive-thru on a traffic artery depends on vehicle counts, not regional growth, which is why the parcel itself still matters more than the metro.

Cost side: labour is the binding constraint

Fast-food wages are $20.00/hour against a $16.90 floor, and the Fast Food Council can raise them. S5 ↗ S4 ↗ Franchise AUV fell 3.8% to $1,874k. S9 ↗ An 8–10% occupancy-cost ratio implies $150k–$187k of annual rent capacity — which is exactly the benchmark used in the model, and the reason a large rent increase at renewal is unlikely to stick.

Capital markets: the cap rate is the swing factor

10-year Treasury 4.90%, policy rate 4.00%. S11 ↗ S12 ↗ Net-lease acquisitions cleared at a 7.3% initial cash yield in 2025. S10 ↗ Each 100bp of cap-rate expansion cuts the exit value by roughly 13–15% at these levels — far more than any rent escalation can recover inside the remaining term.

Tail risk: priced by insurance, not by the cap rate

The 2025 fires are the calibration event: $5.2B–$10.1B of lost output and ~47,000 workers affected. S13 ↗ The parcel's FEMA, fire-hazard, seismic and insurance status are all unverified here because the APN is withheld. Those four items are the first thing to close.

The ULA threshold is a cliff, not a slope

Below $5.4 million, the city tax is 0.45%. At $5.4 million it becomes 4.45%, and at $10.9 million it becomes 5.95%. A sale at exactly the threshold costs roughly $216,000 more than a sale one dollar below it. The thresholds are indexed annually to the BLS Chained CPI, so they move.

Two tax rules that set the timing

Proposition 13 caps the tax while you hold

The taxable value may not rise by more than 2% in any year, as a matter of statute.

Revenue and Taxation Code section 51(a)(1)(D) S12 ↗

Consequence: a long-held asset carries an effective tax rate below the market rate. That gap is real, permanent while held, and disappears on sale, because a sale establishes a new base year. A buyer prices the higher tax bill into the offer. This is a genuine argument for holding that has nothing to do with the local economy.

Section 1031 defers the gain, but only into real property

A qualifying like-kind exchange of real property does not require recognition of gain.

IRS, Like-kind exchanges S14 ↗

Consequence: selling is only a wealth-preserving move if the proceeds go into another real-property position. Cash in a taxable account at the 4.38% one-year Treasury yield does not replace a 6.8% net-lease yield plus appreciation. The exchange is the mechanism, not a detail.

The California top marginal income tax rate is 12.30% S13 ↗. The workbook deliberately does not compute an after-tax figure, because the owner’s basis, depreciation history and filing status are unknown (rows X10–X12).

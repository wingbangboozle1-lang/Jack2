Case for and against each strategy

1. Negotiate an extension at a market reset, then sell into a 1031 if the price clears the trigger

Supports

  • Attacks the only variable the owner controls: rent and term.
  • Selling with a fresh ten-year term removes the rollover discount a buyer would otherwise apply.
  • Keeps the section 1031 route open and defers the tax decision until a replacement exists.

Against

  • Depends on tenant cooperation, and the tenant is in a closure programme.
  • An extension does not reduce concentration risk - it only makes the asset more sellable.
  • Time cost: negotiation, marketing and a 1031 identification is a multi-quarter project.

2. Sell now into a section 1031 exchange

Supports

  • Removes the single-asset earthquake, wildfire and insurance tail in one transaction.
  • Converts one illiquid parcel into a diversified, re-priceable position.
  • Locks in today's cap-rate environment instead of betting it holds to 2032.

Against

  • Crystallises disposition cost including 0.45% city and 0.11% county transfer tax, and Measure ULA above the indexed threshold.
  • Requires an identified replacement inside the exchange window; a failed exchange is worse than not selling.
  • Gives up the Prop 13 assessed-value cap, which is real while held and resets on sale.

3. Hold to expiry and renew at market

Supports

  • Preserves the Prop 13 cap, which a sale resets.
  • No transaction cost, no exchange execution risk, no forced timeline.
  • Retains full optionality until the expiry window opens.

Against

  • Leaves the decision to a negotiation conducted under time pressure.
  • Carries the full single-asset hazard exposure for another six to fourteen years.
  • If the closure programme reaches this unit, the owner holds a vacant drive-through.

4. Renew long-term and hold as a permanent income asset

Supports

  • Maximum contractual certainty of cash flow if the tenant performs.
  • Best tax profile: no recognition event and the Prop 13 cap continues.

Against

  • Maximises concentration in a shrinking county, a contracting sector and one credit.
  • A long lease also blights redevelopment of the land.
  • Scores worst against the owner's stated requirement to keep risk manageable.

Why the answer is a process rather than a pick

The simulation finds selling better than holding in only 26.0% of 20,000 paths, but holding carries a 5th-percentile outcome 23.9% below its mean against 19.4% for the diversified path. When one option wins on the mean and loses in the tail, the correct response is not to pick a side on conviction — it is to change the terms of the choice. A rent reset at renewal does that: it raises the hold case and the sale price simultaneously, because a buyer pays for remaining term as well as for rent.

The honest limitation. The ranking above depends on weights that the analyst chose and on an assumed going-in cap rate. Change the going-in cap and the wealth scores should change with it. The triggers exist precisely so that the owner can replace the assumption with an appraisal without needing to re-run anything.

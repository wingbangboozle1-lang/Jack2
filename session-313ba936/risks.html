Business as usual

These should be priced into every year of ownership. Most of them argue neither for an immediate sale nor for a passive renewal; they argue for pricing correctly and for negotiating protection into the lease.

R01Tenant creditimpact highlikelihood mediumverified basis

Tenant same-store sales keep falling and the franchisee at this location becomes unable to pay contract rent.

Mechanism. System same-store sales were -1.1% in Q3 FY2026 and franchise-level margin fell from 39.3% to 37.4%. A single-restaurant franchisee absorbs closures and weak volume before the parent does.

Mitigation. Read the franchisee's financial statements before any renewal decision; require a corporate or personal guaranty at renewal; consider a security deposit; verify rent coverage from the franchisee's P&L, not from the parent's disclosure.

Official basis. Same-store sales (1.1%) decline, franchise-level margin 37.4%, bad-debt expense higher year over year (S15). S15 ↗

R02Tenant creditimpact highlikelihood mediumverified basis

The brand closes this restaurant as part of a rationalisation programme while the lease is still running.

Mechanism. Restaurant count fell from 2,168 to 2,115 in twelve months, with 17 closures in Q3 alone, attributed in the release to the 'JACK on Track' closure programme.

Mitigation. Obtain the franchise agreement term and the parent's development/closure criteria; negotiate a closure-notice covenant and a termination fee at renewal; keep the land value case underwritten so the site survives vacancy.

Official basis. 2,115 restaurants at 2026-07-05 vs 2,168 a year earlier; 17 closures in the quarter (S15). S15 ↗

R03Lease structureimpact highlikelihood highverified basis

The lease is with a franchisee, not with Jack in the Box Inc., so the parent's balance sheet provides no credit support.

Mechanism. Jack in the Box is overwhelmingly franchised (1,966 of 2,115 units). The parent's cash of $46.3m and $1.43bn of debt do not automatically stand behind a franchisee's rent.

Mitigation. Identify the named tenant and read the guaranty. If there is no parent guaranty, the asset is franchisee-credit, and both the sale price and the hold case must reflect that.

Official basis. Franchised 1,966 of 2,115 units; cash $46,318k; long-term debt $1,428,667k (S15, S16). S16 ↗

R04Demand and demographicsimpact mediumlikelihood highverified basis

The county's trade-area population keeps shrinking, so restaurant volume and therefore rent coverage erode.

Mechanism. County population is -3.2% since the 2020 Census base and the state projects a further 215,911 person decline to 2040. Fewer households is fewer transactions for a limited-service restaurant.

Mitigation. Price the asset on the land and on the site's own traffic, not on county growth. Any renewal should be at or above current rent, never at a discount for 'market' reasons unless a rental survey proves the discount.

Official basis. County population 9,694,934 (-3.2% since 2020) (S02); official projections 9,853,435 (2025) to 9,637,524 (2040) (S03). S03 ↗

Exposed strategies: Hold to expiry then renew Renew and hold long-term

R05Labour costimpact mediumlikelihood highverified basis

Statutory wage floors rise faster than the tenant can reprice, squeezing restaurant margin and rent-paying capacity.

Mechanism. Three overlapping floors now apply: state $16.90 (2026), fast food $20.00 (AB 1228), and City of Los Angeles $18.42 effective 2026-07-01. The ECI for the LA CSA is running +3.9% against +3.1% nationally.

Mitigation. Treat escalations as protection, not as a negotiating gift. A renewal with flat rent in a 3.9% wage-cost environment is a real rent cut.

Official basis. State $16.90 effective 2026-01-01 (S07); fast food $20.00 (S08); City of LA $18.42 effective 2026-07-01 (S09); ECI LA CSA +3.9% (S05). S07 ↗

Mechanism. City median household income is $81,939 and the city poverty rate is 16.5%; both are worse than the county. Limited-service restaurants are the most exposed segment when real incomes compress.

R07Capital marketsimpact highlikelihood mediumverified basis

Cap rates expand and the asset is worth less at expiry than it is today, so waiting to sell destroys value.

Mechanism. The 10-year Treasury is 4.88% and the current net-lease acquisition benchmark is a 6.8% initial cash yield. If either rises, a buyer's required yield rises with it and the price falls.

Mitigation. The model solves for the exit cap at which holding and selling tie, and publishes it, so the decision can be made against an observable number rather than a forecast.

Official basis. 10-year Treasury 4.88% on 2026-11-09 (S01); net-lease initial weighted average cash yield 6.8% in Q3 2026 (S17). S01 ↗

R08Reinvestmentimpact highlikelihood mediumverified basis

Sale proceeds cannot be redeployed at a yield high enough to beat holding.

Mechanism. Selling only wins if the after-cost proceeds earn more than the asset does. That required yield is a solved number in the model, and it is sensitive to the reinvestment assumption.

Mitigation. Do not list without an identified replacement pipeline. A 1031 exchange requires like-kind US real property (S14); cash in a taxable account at 4.38%-4.88% is not a substitute for a 6.8% net-lease yield.

Official basis. Section 1031 relief applies to real property exchanges (S14); T-bill 4.38% and 10-year 4.88% (S01); net-lease benchmark 6.8% (S17). S14 ↗

R09Transaction costimpact mediumlikelihood mediumverified basis

The cost of selling is higher than assumed because the price crosses a Measure ULA threshold.

Mechanism. City transfer tax is 0.45% below $5.4m but 4.45% between $5.4m and $10.9m and 5.95% at or above $10.9m. A sale at $5.4m costs about $240k more than a sale at $5.39m.

Mitigation. Model the after-tax price at the actual negotiated number, not an average rate. If the value lands just above a threshold, the marginal cost of the last dollar is punitive and the negotiation should reflect it.

Official basis. Base 0.45%; ULA 4% above $5,400,000 and 5.5% at $10,900,000 or more, effective for closings after 2026-06-30 (S10). S10 ↗

R10Property taximpact mediumlikelihood highverified basis

Property taxes jump after a sale because the Proposition 13 cap resets to the purchase price.

Mechanism. The 2% annual cap applies to the base-year value while the owner holds. A sale establishes a new base year, so the buyer prices the higher tax bill into the offer, and the seller's own replacement property carries the same reset.

Mitigation. Quantify the owner's effective tax rate as a percentage of value and compare it with the market rate. A long-held asset carries an implicit, transferable-to-nobody subsidy that disappears on sale - this is a genuine reason to hold, and it is a reason a buyer will discount.

Official basis. Annual inflation factor may not exceed 2 percent of the prior year's value (RTC 51(a)(1)(D), S12). S12 ↗

Exposed strategies: Sell now Extend then sell

R11Concentrationimpact highlikelihood highverified basis

One tenant, one parcel, one metro. A single adverse event removes a large share of the owner's net worth.

Mechanism. The asset is a single-tenant building in a single county. Its cash flow and its resale value are driven by the same two variables, tenant performance and local demand.

Mitigation. This is the strongest argument for exchanging rather than holding, and it is independent of cap rates. The Monte Carlo publishes the tail-loss difference between the concentrated and diversified paths rather than a mean difference.

Official basis. The reference net-lease portfolio holds 15,536 properties leased to 1,842 clients (S17) - the diversification the subject owner does not have. S17 ↗

R12Executionimpact highlikelihood mediumverified basis (lease dates unverified)

The owner starts the process too late and loses the option to choose.

Mechanism. A net-lease sale plus a 1031 identification takes months; the lease's option-notice date can fall earlier than the expiry. Running out of time converts a choice into a forced outcome.

Mitigation. Read the option notice clause first. Set a hard decision date at least 12 months before the earliest notice date, not before the expiry date.

Official basis. Section 1031 requires the exchange to be structured as an exchange of real property (S14). The lease's own dates are unverified (L01, L04). S14 ↗

Black swan and tail events

These do not happen most years. When they do, they can remove a large share of a single-asset position at once. This is the section that most directly distinguishes holding from diversifying.

R14Earthquakeimpact very highlikelihood medium (60% over 30 years)verified basis

A damaging earthquake destroys or closes the building, and the loss exceeds insurance recovery.

Mechanism. USGS puts the Los Angeles region at a 60% chance of M6.7+ in 30 years, 46% at M7.0+ and 31% at M7.5+. A drive-through is a lightweight structure, but the land, utilities and customer base are exposed, and business interruption is not covered by a standard property policy.

Mitigation. Obtain a parcel-level CGS seismic/liquefaction screening and a current earthquake quote; if uninsured or underinsured, this single exposure can exceed several years of rent. Diversification removes it; nothing else does.

Official basis. Los Angeles region, 30-year likelihood: M>=6.7 60%, M>=7.0 46%, M>=7.5 31% (USGS UCERF3, S18). S18 ↗

Exposed strategies: Hold to expiry then renew Renew and hold long-term Extend then sell

R15Wildfire and smokeimpact very highlikelihood low to mediumverified basis

A wildfire reaches the urban asset, or smoke and power shut-offs close it for weeks.

Mechanism. The Eaton Fire burned 14,021 acres inside Los Angeles County in January 2025, destroyed 9,419 structures and damaged 1,076. It started on 2025-01-07 and took 24 days to contain. A January fire in an urban-interface county is the relevant precedent, not a mountain-forest scenario.

Mitigation. Look up the parcel's Fire Hazard Severity Zone (CAL FIRE) and confirm the policy does not exclude fire following earthquake; check whether the carrier has withdrawn from the ZIP code. Insurance availability, not the fire itself, is often what destroys value.

Official basis. Eaton Fire: 14,021 acres, 9,419 structures destroyed, 1,076 damaged, 19 fatalities, cause Electrical (CAL FIRE, S20); FHSZ framework and LRA maps (S19). S20 ↗

R16Insurance marketimpact highlikelihood low to mediumverified basis

The property becomes uninsurable or insurable only at a cost that eliminates net income.

Mechanism. After a major loss event, carriers reprice or withdraw. The USGS fact sheet records that quake-risk repricing after Northridge produced an insurance-availability crisis resolved only by creating the California Earthquake Authority.

Mitigation. Obtain a quote now, in writing, with the renewal premium and the deductible. A buyer will do exactly this during diligence, so the owner should know the answer before listing.

Official basis. The 1994 Northridge earthquake contributed to a homeowner-insurance-availability crisis resolved in 1996 by creating the California Earthquake Authority (S18). Current premium is unverified (H11). S18 ↗

R21Legal / titleimpact mediumlikelihood mediumunverified - owner action

An unknown easement, entitlement defect or environmental condition is discovered in a buyer's diligence and reprices or kills the sale.

Mechanism. Drive-through sites commonly carry reciprocal easement agreements, signage easements, or a legacy underground-storage issue. None of these appear in any official dataset; they appear in a title report and a Phase I.

Mitigation. Order title and a Phase I before listing, not after going under contract. A defect found by the buyer costs more than one found by the owner.

The headline is mildly positive: unemployment fell from 5.6% a year ago to 4.8%, and the county added 20,300 jobs. The composition is the problem for a restaurant tenant. Health care and social assistance contributed 37,500 jobs — 83% of net growth — while information lost 10,200 and government lost 7,800. Growth concentrated in one non-cyclical sector is not the same as a broad expansion.

The county is wealthy in aggregate and thinner in the city core. For a limited-service restaurant, the city number is the relevant one: a median household income of $81,939 against a median gross rent of $1,954 leaves limited headroom for price increases, which matters because the tenant’s own margin is already compressing.

Cost pressure on the tenant

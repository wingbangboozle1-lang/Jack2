Executive summary

Hold the option open, then price it

One page: the answer, the three numbers that decide it, and the risks that would change it.

Prepared 2026-11-10. Internal decision support for the owner. Not an appraisal and not tax advice.

1. The question

The asset is a single-tenant Jack in the Box under a net lease in central Los Angeles, expiring somewhere between 2030 and 2035. The owner must decide whether to sell before expiry, extend and then sell, or hold and renew, in a way that preserves wealth and keeps a manageable level of risk.

2. The answer

Negotiate first; let the negotiation set the decision. The evidence does not support a fire sale and it does not support passive renewal. It supports a sequenced plan: obtain the lease and the insurance quote, get an appraisal, open an extension conversation that trades term for a rent reset, and then choose between holding and exchanging on the basis of two observable numbers — the appraised going-in cap rate against the 6.52% breakeven, and the replacement yield against the 7.01% requirement.

Why the risk side breaks the tie

A seeded 20,000-trial simulation with rate shocks, rent uncertainty, renewal probability, earthquake and fire hazards, and partial insurance recovery gives holding the better central outcome (+4.9% of wealth) but a materially worse left tail: the 5th percentile falls 23.9% below the mean when concentrated, against 19.4% for the diversified path. Selling wins in only 26.0% of paths. That is the honest shape of the trade: holding is not wrong, but it is the choice that can hurt the most.

3. The three numbers that decide it

Breakeven going-in cap
6.52%
Above this cap rate (a cheaper sale price) holding wins. Below it, selling wins.
Required replacement yield
7.01%
If the exchange cannot clear this, selling is a downgrade dressed as an exit.
Concession that flips the answer
5.7%
How much rent the tenant must win at renewal for selling to become the better decision.

All three are solved by the engine in session-313ba936/scripts/model.py and reproduce exactly. The breakeven cap has a closed-form check of Y×(1−s) = 6.52%, which the numerical solve matches to 0.000 percentage points.

6. What to do in the next 90 days

  1. Read the lease. Exact expiry, the renewal option and above all the option notice date, the escalations, the recovery structure, and who the named tenant actually is — the franchisee or the parent. Fourteen fields on Flagged for review depend on this one document.
  2. Get the insurance quote in writing. Property, fire, and earthquake, with the deductible. This is the single input most likely to change the answer and it is entirely absent today.
  3. Look up the parcel on three public maps. CAL FIRE Fire Hazard Severity Zone, FEMA flood zone, and a California Geological Survey seismic screening. All three are address lookups; none requires a paid report.
  4. Order a broker opinion of value. The model needs one number from the market — the going-in cap rate — to convert every breakeven on this site into dollars.
  5. Open the extension conversation. Ask for term in exchange for a reset. Do not list first: a property with a near-term rollover is priced for the rollover.
  6. Do not start a 1031 exchange without an identified replacement. The exchange is what makes selling an upgrade; without it, selling is a taxable event plus a transaction cost.

7. What this workbook will not do

  • It will not state the property's value. The rent is unknown, so a value would be an invention. It publishes breakevens instead.
  • It will not quote a capital-gains rate. That depends on the owner's basis, depreciation history and filing status, none of which are known.
  • It will not forecast the local economy as if it were measured. Official county projections are used where they exist and labelled; local forecasts beyond the official series are not used for any value.
  • It will not publish a parcel-level hazard rating. The address is deliberately not held in this repository. The lookups are specified instead.
  • It will not treat an assumption as a fact. Every assumption is tagged and printed with its reason on the Projection model page.

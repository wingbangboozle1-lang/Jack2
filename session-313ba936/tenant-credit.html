Reading it honestly

  • Negative book equity is not a default signal by itself. Jack in the Box has run a leveraged, share-repurchase-heavy capital structure for years. The relevant number for a landlord is not equity — it is the franchisee's rent coverage and the parent's willingness to support the brand at this location.
  • The trend is the signal. Same-store sales negative, unit count falling, 17 closures in a single quarter, and a named closure programme ("JACK on Track") in the company's own release. A closure programme is not a theoretical risk for a specific restaurant; it is a process that produces specific closures.
  • The margin pressure has a cause the lease can fix or worsen. Franchise-level margin fell because of lower sales, fewer restaurants and higher bad-debt expense. At renewal, the landlord and the franchisee are negotiating over exactly the cost that is squeezing that margin.
  • A 103.5% rent recapture rate across a large net-lease portfolio is genuine evidence that net-lease re-leasing has recently been flat-to-positive in general — which is a reason for cautious optimism about the site's re-letting value, not a guarantee for this tenant.

Not published here: a credit rating. Rating-agency opinions are not official government or regulator records, and none was read this session. Publishing a recalled rating would be exactly the kind of unverified claim this workbook is built to avoid, so row T12 is blank.

Decision / Sell vs Renew

Sell Before Expiry — or Renew?

A balanced, trigger-based answer. The goal: preserve wealth, keep measured growth, and never bet the estate on a single lease date. Numbers use placeholders or clearly-labeled illustrations; the framework is the deliverable.

Bottom line. Renew first, sell only on a defined trigger. Specifically: (1) open renewal negotiations in 2026–2027 for a 10–15 year extension with ≥2.5%/yr escalations (or ≥10%/5yr), absolute-NNN with verified insurance, and a corporate/franchisee guarantee; (2) simultaneously get a confidential broker opinion of value and 1031 counsel on standby; (3) sell only if a trigger in the table below fires. Do not let the lease reach 2030–2035 unextended.

Why: after ≈8.6–10.6% transaction friction (ULA + transfer taxes + commissions, before income tax) and ±13–19% value sensitivity per 100 bps of cap rate, a negotiated renewal dominates a pressured sale in almost every scenario — while preserving the option to sell into DTLA 2040 / post-Olympics re-rating later.

Why sell can still be right — honest counterpoints

  • Tenant-default reality. JIB is 93% franchised with falling AUV ($1.949M→$1.874M) and a discontinued dividend to fund debt paydown. If your counterparty is a weak franchisee, "NNN safety" is an illusion — bail early (S3).
  • Cyclical top. If NNN QSR cap rates compress below ~5.5% (e.g., post-Olympics euphoria or rate cuts), selling into the bid and 1031-ing into multi-tenant diversification can be rational (S4).
  • Concentration. One pad = [X]% of net worth. Wealth preservation literally may require selling part of the bet and re-diversifying (S5).
  • Estate design. If heirs can't manage a single-tenant asset, a sale or DST/1031 simplification may beat a "hold forever" purity test.
  • Honest bias check. This framework was built to resist the endowment effect. The trigger table is pre-committed: when a trigger fires, the decision is already made — reducing emotional/recency bias at the critical moment.

Action plan (owner, no other manual work required beyond signature)

  1. Week 1–2: Pull lease abstract; confirm exact expiry, escalations, guarantor (JIB Inc. vs franchisee), insurance schedule, kick-outs. Engage broker for confidential valuation + off-market solicitation of interest (no listing).
  2. Week 2–4: Request estoppel certificate + franchisee financials (or unit AUV for trailing 8 quarters) via tenant; commission property-condition assessment; verify earthquake/fire coverage and loss-of-rents limits.
  3. Week 4–8: Negotiate extension LOI — target 10–15 yrs, ≥2.5%/yr or 10%/5yr bumps, absolute NNN, guarantee/LC, no early kick-out <7 years. Simultaneously brief 1031 qualified intermediary (no commitment).
  4. On LOI term sheet: score against Trigger Matrix (R1–R3 → sign; S1–S5 → execute sale process). Tax counsel models ULA/1031/§1014 for the two paths.
  5. Quarterly monitoring: JACK 10-K/10-Qs (SEC EDGAR), EDD county jobs report, Port of LA TEUs, insurance renewals, and your tenant's sales reports. Trigger check = 30 minutes per quarter.
  6. Hard checkpoints: 2027-06-30 (extension signed?), 2028-12-31 (absorption check: re-rate vs Black swan review), 2030-01-01 (if no extension by now, escalate sales track), 12 months before expiry (final sell/lease-up decision).

Decision

The answer, the four strategies behind it, and the triggers that would change it

Scored on what the client asked for: preserve wealth first, allow growth, keep risk manageable.

Do not choose between 'sell' and 'renew' as a slogan. Run a sequenced plan: strengthen the lease now, price the asset in 2027-2028, and sell into a 1031 exchange only if pricing and the replacement yield clear explicit thresholds.

1. Negotiate an early extension with a rent reset, then sell with a long WALT2. Hybrid: extend 5 years now at a reset rent, keep a 2030-2035 sale window3. Sell now4. Hold to expiry, renew at market, keep the asset

Why

  • The model shows the decision is not dominated by either side. Base case: selling and reinvesting at the verified 7.3% net-lease benchmark beats holding by about $0.25M of 2040 wealth; holding beats selling and reinvesting at the Treasury rate by about $1.36M. The answer turns on the reinvestment yield the owner can actually achieve - the breakeven is 6.97%.
  • Because the margin is thin, the highest-value action is not to sell or to hold, but to change the asset: secure a market-rent reset and a longer term. That is the only path that improves both the sell price and the hold economics.
  • Everything that could move the answer by more than the thin margin is a fact the owner has not yet supplied: actual rent, exact expiry, basis, debt, and the parcel's hazard classification. So the plan below is trigger-based, not a blind instruction.

The four strategies, scored

D1 · weighted score 3.80 / 5

Sell now (2026-2027) and 1031-exchange into diversified net lease

Capture today's pricing before the short-WALT discount appears, defer gain with a Section 1031 exchange, and replace one tenant and one parcel with several.

Arguments for

  • Removes single-tenant, single-parcel, single-metro concentration - the largest risk in the file (RK20).
  • Section 1031 defers the tax that would otherwise consume a large share of proceeds (S16, RK19).
  • Verified net-lease deployment benchmark is 7.3% (S10) versus the 6.97% reinvestment yield the base case needs to match holding - a thin but positive margin.
  • Avoids the short-WALT buyer pool problem entirely (RK05).

Arguments against

  • Gives up a 2% Prop 13 assessment cap in a 3.7% CPI environment (S6, S1) - a real, permanent benefit that is not transferable.
  • Forfeits the option value of a market-rent reset at 2030-2035 if contract rent is below market (RK03).
  • Execution risk: 45/180-day exchange identification rules (S16) and a 3.5%+ cost stack including city/county transfer taxes (S7, S8).
  • If the asset is actually worth more than the benchmark suggests, selling now may leave value on the table.

D2 · weighted score 3.95 / 5

Negotiate an early extension with a rent reset, then sell with a long WALT

Use the 2030-2035 expiry as leverage: trade the tenant a 10-20 year extension for a market rent reset and a credit guaranty, then sell the re-priced asset into the deepest possible buyer pool.

Arguments for

  • Creates the value that D1 gives up: a market reset plus a long lease is the highest-priced version of this asset (RK03, RK05).
  • The tenant has structural incentive - its franchise term is 20 years and it reprices renewals to market anyway (S9).
  • Selling after the reset removes the short-WALT discount and attracts 1031 buyers who need term.
  • Keeps the 2% assessment cap (S6) while the extension is negotiated.

Arguments against

  • Requires the tenant to cooperate; a franchisee facing a $20/hour fast-food wage floor (S5) and a 3.8% AUV decline (S9) may not be able to absorb a higher rent.
  • Two-step execution (negotiate, then market) with timing risk on rates (S11).
  • Extending a lease you then sell can be seen by buyers as a captured premium rather than a gift.

D3 · weighted score 3.30 / 5

Hold to expiry, renew at market, keep the asset

Do nothing until the option window, then reprice at market and continue collecting NNN rent with an inflation-capped tax basis.

Arguments for

  • Model base case: holding beats selling and reinvesting at the Treasury rate by about $1.36M of 2040 wealth, and beats it in every scenario tested.
  • Beats selling and reinvesting at the 7.3% net-lease benchmark in the bull case by about $0.43M (model output).
  • No transaction cost, no tax event, no reinvestment risk, and the Prop 13 basis persists (S6).
  • Zero action required until the option window opens - the cheapest path administratively.

Arguments against

  • In the bear case, holding loses about $0.69M versus selling and reinvesting at the net-lease benchmark.
  • Concentration risk compounds over 6-14 more years (RK20).
  • Wildfire, earthquake and insurance shocks are unhedged by diversification (RK12, RK13, RK15).
  • Leaves the owner exposed to a single franchisee's credit (RK02).

D4 · weighted score 3.90 / 5

Hybrid: extend 5 years now at a reset rent, keep a 2030-2035 sale window

Take a short extension with a fair-market reset and a strengthened guaranty, then decide at the new expiration with full information and a re-priced asset.

Arguments for

  • Locks in a rent reset (the model needs only +7.1% uplift to beat selling into the net-lease benchmark) while preserving the sale option.
  • Avoids both the short-WALT discount and the permanent loss of the Prop 13 basis.
  • Buys time to complete parcel-level hazard and insurance work that is currently unverified (M82-M85).
  • Limits downside: a 5-year extension is short enough to reprice again in a better market.

Arguments against

  • A short extension still leaves a short WALT at the next decision point.
  • Requires tenant consent and a guaranty the franchisee may not be able to give.
  • Splits the difference on every axis - the classic risk of a compromise.

For you as an individual, not just for the asset

  • Wealth preservation first: a single-tenant, single-parcel, single-metro asset worth roughly $2.0M-$2.7M at the benchmark rent is a concentrated position. Diversification is the only free risk reduction available, and a 1031 exchange is the tax-efficient way to get it (S16).
  • Do not sell for the sake of selling: at the benchmark rent the asset prices below the $5.4M Measure ULA threshold, so the 4%/5.5% surtax does not bite, and the transfer-tax cost stack is about 0.56% plus brokerage (S7, S8). The friction is real but not disqualifying.
  • Keep the option alive: a 5-year extension preserves both the tax basis (S6) and the sale option, which is why D4 ranks second rather than last.
  • Reserve, then reinvest: whatever the decision, hold a 12-month NOI reserve outside the property. Every black-swan scenario in the register ends in a liquidity problem before it ends in a valuation problem.

What would make this recommendation wrong

  • If the actual rent is much higher than the benchmark. Then the asset may cross the $5.4M Measure ULA threshold, the cost stack changes, and the price-test around the threshold becomes the dominant decision. S8 ↗
  • If the lease has a fixed-price option. A below-market fixed option rent at the 2030–2035 window would make renewal cheap for the tenant and expensive for you — and would flip the ranking toward selling before the option is exercised.
  • If the parcel is in a very-high fire hazard or mapped liquefaction zone. Insurance and engineering conclusions could override the financial ranking entirely. Both are currently unverified (M82–M85).
  • If the owner needs liquidity or cannot tolerate concentration. Then D1 rises to first regardless of the modelled spread — a risk preference is not an error in the arithmetic.

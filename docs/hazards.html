Geography & catastrophe risk

The black-swan layer is not hypothetical — it happened nine months ago

California lost 16,627 structures to wildfire in 2025. Four Los Angeles County incidents exceeded 5,000 acres, two of them starting on the same day. This page separates what is verified from what cannot be determined without a parcel identifier, and explains how each hazard actually reaches a net-lease landlord.

The asymmetry: selling transfers catastrophe exposure to the buyer at closing. Holding retains it in full, while the recovery upside stays with the buyer either way.

Why a NNN landlord should care at all

A common error: "it's NNN, the tenant pays insurance and taxes, so hazards are the tenant's problem." That is wrong in four specific ways.

Transmission channelHow it reaches the landlordMitigation
Insurance availability and priceEven where the tenant pays, a doubling of premium is a direct hit to the tenant's P&L and therefore to its willingness and ability to renew at the same rent. In severe cases coverage becomes unobtainable, making the site uninsurable and unfinanceable.Confirm the lease's insurance covenant and who bears premium escalation
Post-event demand destructionA regional fire or earthquake displaces households and closes roads for months. QSR sales fall immediately; a marginal store can go cash-negative.Trade-area diversification; no single-asset concentration
Physical loss / business interruptionIf the landlord owns roof and structure, rebuild is a landlord cost and rent abatement runs concurrently.Confirm absolute-net vs modified; confirm landlord insurance
Buyer-pool contractionAfter a visible catastrophe, buyers underwrite the whole submarket wider. Cap rates move even for undamaged buildings.Sell before the repricing, or into a market that has already repriced

Hazard is not risk. A Fire Hazard Severity Zone measures physical conditions producing likelihood and expected fire behaviour over a 30–50 year horizon, ignoring mitigation such as home hardening or fuel reduction. A dense, flat, urban central-LA parcel is typically in the Local Responsibility Area and usually Moderate or unclassified, whereas hillside parcels in the same county are Very High. That is an inference, not a verified fact for this parcel — and it is not zero, because smoke, power shut-offs, insurance repricing and buyer sentiment are county-wide.

Earthquake: the risk diversification inside LA cannot fix

Southern California's seismic exposure is regional and correlated: one event affects the entire buyer pool, the tenant's whole store fleet and the entire insurance market simultaneously. That is the definition of a risk that diversifying inside the region cannot remove — which is an argument about the portfolio, independent of this parcel.

The specific fault proximity, seismic design category, and liquefaction zone cannot be determined without the address and are left blank on the Flagged page. The authoritative sources are the USGS Earthquake Hazards Program and the California Geological Survey.

The asymmetry that decides the hold-versus-sell question

Sell nowHold 4–9 years to expiry
Exposure to a catastropheTransferred to the buyer at closingRetained in full
Who bears insurance repricingBuyerOwner
Who bears the demand shockBuyerOwner
Who keeps the recovery upside if the event is benignBuyerOwner

Holding is a long position in the survival of one parcel's cash flow through a period in which the historical frequency of severe LA County events is visibly rising. That is defensible only if it is paid for — i.e. only if the value of waiting, plus the tax deferral from not selling, exceeds the option value of de-risking now. The projection engine quantifies exactly that trade-off, and the answer is no.

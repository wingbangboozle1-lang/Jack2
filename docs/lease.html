Lease & term

The lease is the asset — and nobody has read it

The Owner describes an NNN lease expiring between 2030 and 2035. That description is the entire basis for every valuation assumption in this dossier, and it has not been verified. Eight specific clauses change the answer, and six of them are worth more than a percentage point of cap rate each.

Reading the lease is worth ~$500,000 (21.6% of value, on a $200,000-NOI asset) and costs nothing. Nothing else in this dossier should be acted on before it is done.

The pricing identity

Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate. Only two things can move value: NOI, or the cap rate. A NNN lease fixes the landlord's NOI contractually, so before expiry there is almost no lever on the numerator. Remaining term feeds directly into the denominator — net-lease buyers pay for durability of cash flow. Time is therefore a cost, not neutral.

What "NNN" does and does not guarantee

Triple-net typically obliges the tenant to pay base rent plus its share of property taxes, insurance and common-area maintenance. What materially changes value — and what is unverified here — is everything below.

A tenant extension is not a courtesy — it is the single largest value-creation event available. It is also the one thing the tenant will charge for. The model assumes a 4% rent concession and only a 55% chance the franchisor signs at all; negotiate against the 25.2% it creates, and walk away from demands above roughly 15%.

What to obtain, in order

  1. Lease and all amendments (redacted is fine — no personal information needs to enter this repository).
  2. Estoppel certificate from the tenant.
  3. Current rent roll and CAM reconciliations.
  4. Most recent property tax bill and insurance binder (confirm who pays).
  5. Guaranty and any franchisee financial statements.

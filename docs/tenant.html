Tenant & brand

Jack in the Box Inc.: a deleveraging brand that is closing stores

SEC filings only. In a single-tenant NNN deal the landlord does not run a restaurant — it owns a claim on one operator's rent, and that claim fails in two ways: the store stops working, or the operator runs out of money. JACK's own filings show both modes are currently live.

This does not make the asset unsellable. It sets the price and the probability of renewal. It is also a reason to price the asset against the franchisee-QSR cap rate (6.85%) rather than the corporate rate (5.85%).

What the corporate actions tell you

The Del Taco round trip is the cleanest read on management's priorities: a ~$585 million acquisition liquidated for ~$119 million three years later, alongside eliminated dividends and a shrinking store base. That is a deleveraging, retrenching strategy, executed under a programme called "JACK on Track" that explicitly included block restaurant closures.

Why retrenchment helps the landlord

An asset-light JACK needs franchised units to generate royalty streams. Closing a profitable, rent-paying central-LA store is value-destructive to the franchisor too, so a rational franchisor protects viable urban units.

Why it hurts the landlord

The block-closure programme proves the company will close stores in bulk when unit economics disappoint — and closures are decided at brand level, not by the landlord. The store base fell 2.4% in four quarters.

The most valuable unknown: is there a corporate guaranty? 93.0% of the system is franchised (1,966 of 2,115 units), so the tenant of record is likely a franchisee — but that is an inference, not a verified fact for this parcel. Boulder Group prices franchisee QSR paper at 6.85% against 5.85% for corporate credit: a 100 bp spread worth roughly 15% of value. Confirm this before pricing the asset.

Not verifiable in this session

  • Site-level AUV and P&L for PROP-001 — the single best predictor of whether the store renews. Requires the franchisee's redacted P&L or franchisor site data.
  • Whether the tenant of record is corporate or franchisee.
  • Whether a corporate guaranty exists.
  • The franchisee's own financial condition — franchisee distress is the modal default path in QSR net lease.
  • A Jack-in-the-Box-specific net-lease cap rate — Boulder's Q3 2026 Tenant Profiles covers 88 operators but a JACK-specific range was not retrieved.

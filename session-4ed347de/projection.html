3. The flip point

Sell wealth rises with the replacement yield; hold wealth does not depend on it. Solving for the crossing point gives 6.975%. The distance between the two official yields in evidence (7.3% and 6.8%) is 50 basis points, and it is wide enough to move you from one side of that line to the other. See the full grid on Decision.

4. Monte Carlo with black-swan hazards

20,000 independent paths, seed 424242, so this table is exactly reproducible. Every path draws an exit cap, an escalation rate, a renewal outcome and a replacement yield, and then absorbs earthquakes, wildfires and tenant credit events over 2026–2040. The sell path is not modelled as risk-free: the reinvested book takes the same hazards at a 0.40 severity factor.

Selling beats holding in 46.1% of paths; holding beats selling in 53.9%. That is the honest headline: on terminal wealth the two strategies are not distinguishable. The difference in the mean is $0.03M — under 0.5% — and on a risk-adjusted (certainty-equivalent) basis holding is ahead by $0.02M. Neither gap is a strategy.

5. Where the strategies actually differ: the tail

This is the part that the mean hides, and it is the reason the recommendation is not "flip a coin".

Base asset value used for the percentages: $2,307,692. A median path suffers no hazard loss at all in either case — the losses are genuinely tail events, and that is exactly why they are easy to ignore and expensive to ignore. The single-asset book has the same probability of being hit but a much worse outcome when it is.

Honesty about the hazard parameters. The earthquake annual probability (3.008%) is derived from the USGS 60%-in-30-years figure S23 ↗ by assuming events are independent — a simplification, since earthquakes cluster on fault systems. Loss severities and insurance recovery ranges are analyst judgements informed by the January 2025 Eaton Fire outcome (9,419 structures destroyed S24 ↗), not measurements. They are published here so you can replace them, and the workbook is regenerated by rerunning one script.

Risks

Business-as-usual and black-swan risk, each with a remedy — and the two that no lease clause can fix.

How risk is treated here. Each risk is classified by likelihood and by severity, and — critically — each says what to do about it. A risk register that lists hazards without remedies is a worry list. The two that cannot be hedged by any lease clause are uninsurability and concentration; everything else has a contractual answer.

Black-swan risks

These are the events a deterministic scenario table cannot hold: low probability, high severity, and the reason "hold and collect rent" is not a risk-free strategy. Each is either priced in the Monte Carlo or named as a decision trigger.

IDRisk and evidenceLikelihoodSeverityWhat to doSource
B01Earthquake (M6.7 or greater in the region)
USGS UCERF3: 60% for M6.7+, 46% for M7.0+, 31% for M7.5+ within 30 years. In the simulation the single-asset path shows a 58% 99th-percentile loss after modelled insurance recovery.
60% in 30 yearsSevereSeismic retrofit review; earthquake cover priced explicitly; treat uninsurability as a sell trigger.S23 ↗
B02Wildfire reaching the parcel
The January 2025 Eaton Fire destroyed 9,419 structures and killed 19 people in Los Angeles County. Central Los Angeles is not a wildland interface, but the insurance market repriced county-wide afterwards.
Low, but demonstratedSevereVerify the CAL FIRE FHSZ class for the parcel, and above all verify that cover is available at all.S24 ↗
B03Insurance market withdrawal
Post-2025, the binding constraint in Los Angeles is often not the hazard itself but the availability and price of cover. An uninsured NNN building is a different asset class.
RisingSevereObtain a quote now, not at the decision point. A quote is the cheapest risk analysis available.none — qualitative
B04Tenant bankruptcy or mass closure programme
The tenant has an active closure programme and negative book equity. A rejected lease in bankruptcy returns the building with no rent and a re-tenanting cost.
Low to mediumHighGuaranty, security deposit, and a re-tenanting reserve. Confirm whether the lease is a true NNN or modified NNN — the answer changes who pays for what after a vacancy.S09 ↗ S08 ↗
B05Local demand shock (studio or port disruption)
The metro's information sector is contracting and the port complex is a concentration. A single-employer shock propagates through lunch-time traffic.
MediumMediumDiversification solves this better than any lease clause.S02 ↗
B06Rate shock on reinvestment timing
If rates rise between sale and reinvestment, the 180-day exchange window can force capital into a worse yield than the model assumed.
MediumMediumLine up replacement candidates before listing; do not start the clock on an empty pipeline.S17 ↗ S10 ↗
B07Legislative change to 1031 or to transfer taxes
Section 1031 has been a recurring target. A repeal would change the arithmetic of selling entirely.
LowHighIf an exchange is the plan, execute while the law is as it is today.S17 ↗
B08Pandemic-type closure of dine-in or drive-thru
Demonstrated in 2020. A limited-service format with a drive-thru is more resilient than dine-in, which is an argument for the site and against the tenant.
LowHighConfirm drive-thru entitlement status: it is the site's residual value if the tenant leaves.none — qualitative

Risks this analysis cannot quantify

  • Parcel-level hazard status — flood zone, FHSZ class and seismic zone all require the parcel identifier, which is deliberately withheld. Published as blank rows on Verified facts.
  • Insurance pricing — unobtainable without the owner's cooperation and a broker. This is the highest-value unknown in the file.
  • The franchisee's own finances — not public. The franchisor's filings are a proxy, and a decent one, but not the obligor.

Method & privacy

How every number here was produced, what was deliberately withheld, and how to regenerate the whole workbook.

How the work was done

  1. Claimed scope on the shared board first. COORDINATION.md records this session's file scope before any file was written, and the claim was committed and pushed immediately. Eight prior sessions' work is preserved untouched.
  2. Read the primary record, not a summary. Every value was read from the publisher's own page — BLS, Census, BEA, SEC EDGAR, FRED, the California DOF, DIR, FTB, the Legislature, USGS, CAL FIRE and the City and County of Los Angeles. Where a page printed a publication or "updated" date, that date was recorded alongside the value.
  3. Wrote the value down, or wrote nothing. If the primary record could not be reached, the row was left blank and flagged. This is why 16 of 98 rows carry no value.
  4. Re-implemented the published model independently. Not to catch the other sessions out — to establish that the numbers are reproducible. All 21 checked figures matched, which is what makes it legitimate to then vary one input and report what happens.
  5. Modelled the tail, not just the central case. 20,000 seeded paths with earthquake, wildfire and tenant-default hazards on both the hold and the sell path. The sell path absorbs the same hazards at a diversification factor, because pretending a 1031 exchange is risk-free would be the easiest way to produce a wrong answer.
  6. Audited the repository line by line and published the exceptions, including the ones that argue against this session's own conclusion.

Privacy: what is deliberately absent

No personal information appears in this workbook, and none is required to use it. The owner is never identified. The property is described only as a single-tenant net-leased Jack in the Box restaurant in Central Los Angeles within a 2030–2035 lease-expiry window.

WithheldWhyEffect on the analysis
Owner name, entity, contact detailsPersonal information. Never collected, never used, never published. None. The analysis is about the asset and the decision, not the person.
Property street address and parcel number (APN)An APN plus a public records search can identify an owner. It is the single most re-identifying field available. Parcel-level hazard and insurance verification cannot be completed here. Published as blank rows H07–H10 and named as a decision trigger.
Exact contract rent, expiry date, basis, debtCommercially sensitive and not supplied. Every model substitutes an assumption, and each assumption is labelled as one. Nothing is presented as fact.
Franchisee identity and its financial statementsNot public, and identifying it would identify the site. The franchisor's SEC filings are used as a proxy for credit quality. That proxy is stated, not hidden.

A repository-wide scan for email addresses, telephone numbers, street addresses and parcel numbers was run across all 1 of this workbook's files and returns one hit in the wider repository: a public school district's headquarters street address in an earlier session's research notes. The address itself is not reproduced here, so that this workbook stays clean under its own scan. That is a public agency address, not personal information; it is logged as F10 on Flagged for review rather than silently altered in another session's file.

python3 session-4ed347de/scripts/verify.py      # source links, blank-value rule, PII, internal links

The model is deterministic except the Monte Carlo, which is seeded (424242) and therefore also deterministic. Two runs produce byte-identical output.

Limitations, stated plainly

  • This is a decision framework, not an appraisal. It contains no valuation of the building.
  • It is not tax, legal or investment advice. The section 1031, Measure ULA and Proposition 13 material is a summary of published rules with citations, not an opinion on your situation.
  • The eight owner facts would change the analysis materially. They are not present.
  • Parcel-level hazard and insurance status is unverified and could override the financial ranking entirely.
  • Hazard loss severities and insurance recovery ranges are analyst judgements, published in full so they can be challenged.

Decision

Four strategies, one breakeven that both official yields straddle, and the triggers that should change your mind.

The core arithmetic. "Sell and reinvest" produces wealth that rises with the yield you can actually buy. "Hold" produces wealth that does not depend on it. They cross at 6.97%. Both of the official net-lease yields in evidence straddle that line: 7.3% (FY2025) sits above it, 6.8% (Q3 2026) sits below it. That is the whole decision compressed into one number you can go and find out.

Strategies, scored

Scores are analyst judgements on a 1–4.5 scale weighted for wealth preservation (35%), growth (25%), execution risk (20%), liquidity (10%) and tax efficiency (10%). They are not measurements, and they are shown with their reasoning so you can disagree with them.

S1Sell now into a 1031 exchange
Capture today's price, defer gain, and break the single-asset concentration immediately.

  • Defers tax; the 45/180-day regime is available and the asset is far below the Measure ULA tier.

  • Gives away the rent reset that the 2030–2035 window can convert into price.

S2Renew at market, hold indefinitely
Take the option rent, repricing at market, and keep collecting.

  • No transaction cost and no tax event.
  • Beats selling whenever the replacement yield is below the breakeven.
  • Keeps a 57.9% 99th-percentile hazard tail on a single parcel.
  • Keeps full exposure to one franchisee with negative book equity.
  • Zero optionality: no reset negotiation, no price discovery.
S3Extend with a reset, then price the asset
Trade the tenant term for a fair-market reset and a guaranty; sell the re-priced asset into a deeper buyer pool.
 4.10
  • Raises both the sale price and the hold economics — the only option that improves both sides.
  • Preserves the Prop 13 basis while the negotiation runs.
  • Turns time into information: the appraisal arrives before the sale decision.
  • The tenant's own renewal policy is to reprice to market, so the ask is aligned, not adversarial.
  • Requires tenant consent and a guaranty the franchisee may not be able to give.
  • Two-step execution: negotiate, then market, with rate timing risk between the steps.
  • A weak tenant could use the negotiation to delay rather than commit.
S4Extend 5 years only, keep every option open
A shorter extension with a reset, then a second decision at the new expiry.
 3.75
  • Locks a reset while preserving the sale option and the tax basis.
  • Buys time to close the parcel-level hazard and insurance gaps.
  • Leaves a short WALT at the next decision point.
  • Defers the concentration problem instead of solving it.
  • Splits the difference on every axis without a clear reason to.

Why the plan is "extend, then price" rather than either end point

S1 and S2 are the ends of a spectrum whose middle is unknown. S3 is not a compromise between them; it is the only strategy that changes the inputs instead of betting on them. Concretely:

ActionEffect on the sell priceEffect on the hold case
Market rent reset at the 2030–2035 windowRaises NOI, and therefore price at a given cap rateRaises the renewed rent — the published model needs only about +7% uplift to clear the net-lease comparison
Ten-year term instead of an expiry riskRemoves the short-WALT discount and widens the buyer pool to 1031 buyers who need termRemoves the vacancy and downtime risk from the hold case
A guarantyImproves the credit story a buyer underwritesDirectly offsets the tenant's negative book equity
An appraisal obtained before sellingReplaces an assumed cap rate with an observed oneTells you whether to sell at all

Decision triggers

ObservationAction
Appraisal cap rate ≤ 6.0%Sell and exchange into diversified net lease.
Appraisal cap rate > 7.0%Hold, extend, and re-test in 24 months.
A replacement book is genuinely available at ≥ 7.0%Sell and exchange; the arithmetic now favours it.
Tenant signs a 10-year extension at market with a guarantyTake it, then market the asset with long WALT (S3).
Renewal option fixes rent below marketDo not extend. Market the asset before the discount deepens.
Insurance exceeds ~1.5% of value, or is unavailableSell trigger regardless of cap rate.
Parcel in a very-high fire hazard or liquefaction zoneReduce hold bias; engineering clarity before any extension.

The risk of being wrong

If the true replacement yield is 6.8% and you sell anyway, you give up roughly $0.13M of 2040 wealth to buy tail protection worth up to $0.12M in expected hazard losses, plus liquidity and sleep. If the true yield is 7.3% and you hold, you leave $0.25M on the table. Both mistakes are the same size. That is why the answer is "buy information, then decide", not "pick a side now".

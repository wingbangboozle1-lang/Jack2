**Why this matters for a Jack in the Box.** QSR revenue is, to first order, `traffic × ticket × capture rate`. Traffic is a function of residential density and daytime population. A county that has shed **322,480 residents (−3.2 %)** since the 2020 base is a county shedding customers. That is before the price/income effect. A store in central LA is not in a growth market — it is in a **mature, slowly contracting** market. This is the base case against which everything else must be argued.

*Note:* the Owner's brief asked about "population growth". The verified answer is that county population is **declining** and city population is **roughly flat to slightly down**. Any projection that assumes growth would be contradicted by the official record.

| Median monthly owner cost, with mortgage | $3,160 | $3,497 | ACS 2020-2024 | Census QuickFacts | verified |

**Interpretation.** A $90,112 county median income is high in absolute terms, but it must be read against a $834,200 median home value and $1,954 median rent — a **price-to-income ratio of ~9.3×** and rent consuming ~26 % of a single earner's income before utilities. Households in the lower half of this distribution are **structurally squeezed**, which is precisely the JACK customer. JACK's own management commentary in FY2025 attributed sales weakness to lower-income and Hispanic consumers pulling back — and in Q3 FY2026 reported they had stabilised, not recovered.

That is the crux of the demand thesis: the trade area's customers are **not growing in number and are under real income pressure**, so NOI growth must come from *share* or *price*, not from *volume*.

**This is the most important economic finding in the report.** The Los Angeles metro has added **45,400 jobs in seven years — 0.10 % per year**. For scale, the labour force is roughly 5 million, so the metro has effectively been at **full stall** for most of a decade. Employment is also **1.2 % below its October 2024 peak**.

The unemployment rate of 4.5 % looks healthy only in isolation. Read alongside a shrinking population and flat payrolls, the benign reading is that **people have left the labour force**, not that hiring is strong.

**Consequence for the property:** the tenant's sales growth will not get help from job growth. A central-LA QSR must take share from other restaurants. That caps NOI growth at roughly inflation *minus* a haircut — and it caps how much a renewal is worth to the tenant.

There is plenty of demand to capture — the question is not "is there a market" but "will this particular box keep its share". Note the county's accommodation & food services sales were **$44.0 bn** in 2022, so a single unit's ~$1.5–2.5 m of sales is a rounding error in a market this deep — which is exactly why a departing tenant can be replaced, given time and re-leasing capital.

| High school graduate or higher (25+) | 80.9 % | 79.6 % | ACS 2020-2024 | Census QuickFacts | verified |

**What this supports and what it does not.** Educational attainment of 36 % bachelor's-plus is above the national figure and is positive for daytime professional traffic. However:

- **School *quality* is unverified for this property.** The brief asked about school systems. Because no address was retained, the assigned attendance area cannot be determined, so no school-quality claim is made. See Flagged for Review.
- **School quality matters far less for a QSR than for a residential asset.** Its main channel is household formation and family density, which is why the 19.2 % under-18 share is reported — it is the more relevant number.

## 4.6 Synthesis: the trade-area verdict

| Driver | Verified direction | Weight in QSR demand | Verdict for `PROP-001` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Population | **Declining** (county −3.2 %, city −0.8 % since 2020) | Very high | **Negative** |
| Job growth | **Flat** (+0.10 %/yr over 7 yrs; −1.2 % from peak) | High | **Negative/neutral** |
| Unemployment | 4.5 % (back to 2019 levels) | Medium | **Neutral** |
| Household income | $90,112 county median, but 9.3× price-to-income | High | **Neutral** |
| Retail density / market depth | $22,010 retail sales per capita | High | **Positive** |
| Educational attainment | 36.0 % bachelor's+ | Low | **Mild positive** |
| Household size | 2.81 county | Low | **Neutral** |
| Daytime employment density | Cannot verify at parcel level | High | **Unverified** |

**Net: the trade area does not support a growth thesis.** It supports a *stability* thesis — a mature, deep, dense market that will keep a well-located QSR box productive, but will not lift sales enough to justify holding a decaying lease for a premium. This is the analytical hinge for the recommendation in `06`.

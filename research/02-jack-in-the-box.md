## 2.1 Why brand health matters to a net-lease landlord

In a single-tenant NNN deal the landlord does not run a restaurant — it owns a claim on one operator's rent. That claim has two failure modes:

1. **Unit-level failure** — the trade area stops supporting the store; the operator closes rather than renews.
2. **Operator-level failure** — the franchisee, or the franchisor behind the brand, runs out of money.

JACK's own filings show both modes are currently live. That does not make the asset unsellable; it sets the *price* and the *probability of renewal*.

### Interpretation (analysis, not fact)
The Del Taco round-trip is the cleanest read on management: a $585 M acquisition liquidated for $119 M three years later, alongside eliminated dividends and a shrinking store base. That is a **deleveraging, retrenching** strategy. For a landlord, retrenchment cuts both ways:

- **Supportive:** an asset-light JACK needs *franchised* units to generate royalty streams. Closing a profitable, rent-paying central-LA store is value-destructive to the franchisor too, so a rational franchisor protects viable urban units.
- **Adverse:** the "JACK on Track" block-closure programme proves the company will close stores in bulk when unit economics disappoint. Closures are decided at the brand level, not the landlord's. The store base fell from 2,168 to 2,115 (−2.4 %) in four quarters.
